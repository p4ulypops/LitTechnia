## 1. Patricia C. Wrede — "Fantasy Worldbuilding Questions"

**Framework description (verbatim from the SFWA reprint):** "The following list of questions is meant to aid authors of fantasy fiction who are seeking to create believable imaginary settings for their stories. While many of these questions may be helpful or crucial to certain stories, they will not all apply to every story. It is not necessary for an author to answer all, or even any, of the questions in order to start writing, (or to finish writing, either). The idea is simply to provoke people into thinking about the ways their settings and backgrounds hang together … or don't. If it's useful, use it. If not, don't." — [SFWA, Patricia C. Wrede tag page](https://sfwa.org/tag/patricia-c-wrede/)

The list is organised into six category pages: The World; Physical and Historical Features; Magic and Magicians; Peoples and Customs; Social Organization; Commerce, Trade and Public Life; Daily Life. The "optional, dismissible, never mandatory" framing Lit Technica wants is *exactly* Wrede's own stated stance ("If it's useful, use it. If not, don't."), which makes her the strongest precedent to cite.

### Geography / physical world
- "Are the laws of nature and physics actually different in this world, or are they the same as in real life? How does magic fit in? How do magical beasts fit in?" — [Wrede, The World (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-the-world/)
- "How does this world differ physically from earth? Is it the same size (same density, same gravity), same ratio of land/water, same atmosphere, etc.? Does it have more than one sun or moon? Rings?" — [Wrede, The World (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-the-world/)
- "In which geographical areas will the story take place? How much ground will the story cover? What are the most striking features of landscape, climate, animals, etc. in this area? How will these features affect travel time, communication, etc.?" — [Wrede, Physical and Historical Features (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-physical-and-historical-features/)
- "Have human activities affected climate, landscape, etc. in various regions? How? (Example: Sahara Desert growth has been increased by over-farming.)" — [Wrede, Physical and Historical Features (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-physical-and-historical-features/)
- "Which resources (e.g., coal, oil, iron ore, gold, diamonds, limestone, etc.) are particularly abundant, and in which areas? Which are scarce? Are there places where there are rich deposits that haven't been discovered yet…?" — [Wrede, Physical and Historical Features (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-physical-and-historical-features/)
- "What water resources available, and for what uses (a mill wheel requires flowing water, i.e., river or stream; irrigation needs a large, dependable water source like a lake or *large* river; etc.)?" — [Wrede, Physical and Historical Features (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-physical-and-historical-features/)

### History
- "How far back are there records or tales of historical events? How widely known are these stories?" — [Wrede, Physical and Historical Features (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-physical-and-historical-features/)
- "Do average people believe old tales, or do they dismiss some that have a basis in fact (e.g., Troy)?" — [Wrede, Physical and Historical Features (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-physical-and-historical-features/)
- "Who are the heroes and villains of each country's history (e.g., Washington and Lincoln in the U.S.; Henry V in England, etc.)? Why are they heroes/villains and what does this say about the country and the people who admire them?" — [Wrede, Physical and Historical Features (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-physical-and-historical-features/)
- "Which peoples/countries/races have been in conflict in the *recent* past? Why? When and why was the most recent war? Who won?" — [Wrede, Physical and Historical Features (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-physical-and-historical-features/)

### Magic (rules / cost / limits)
- "What things can magic *not* do? What are the limits to magical power? How do magicians try to get around these limits?" — [Wrede, Magic and Magicians (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-magic-and-magicians/)
- "What is the price magicians must pay in order to be magicians — years of study, permanent celibacy, using up bits of their life or memory with each spell, etc.? Does anyone ever try to get around the price of magic?" — [Wrede, Magic and Magicians (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-magic-and-magicians/)
- "Where does magic power come from: the gods, the 'mana' of the world, the personal willpower of the magician? Is magic an exhaustible resource?" — [Wrede, Magic and Magicians (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-magic-and-magicians/)
- "How long does it take to cast a spell? Can spells be stored for later, instant use? Does working spells take lots of long ritual, or is magic a 'point and shoot' affair?" — [Wrede, Magic and Magicians (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-magic-and-magicians/)
- "Is there a difference between miracles and magic? If so, how are they distinguished?" — [Wrede, Magic and Magicians (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-magic-and-magicians/)
- "Can spells and/or magical items be mass-produced? Are there magic carpet factories and boutiques selling magic rings?" — [Wrede, Magic and Magicians (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-magic-and-magicians/)
- "To what degree does the presence of magic, magical objects, and wizards replace technology (for example, a chest that is enchanted to keep its contents cold could replace the refrigerator)? Duplicate technology? Supplement technology?" — [Wrede, Magic and Magicians (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-magic-and-magicians/)

### Government / law
- "What is the basic style of government: feudal, aristocratic, oligarchy, absolute ruler, democracy, what? What forms are used in neighboring countries, and why are they the same or different?" — [Wrede, Social Organization (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-social-organization/)
- "Who has the right to levy taxes? For what? On what or whom? Can taxes be paid in kind, or do certain things require money?" — [Wrede, Social Organization (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-social-organization/)
- "Who will take over running the government if the current head of state is incapacitated? How is this determined? Is there an heir apparent (either actual or political)? What happens if the heir is a minor?" — [Wrede, Social Organization (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-social-organization/)
- "What things are considered truly serious crimes and why? (E.g., in a trade-oriented culture, counterfeiting might be a death-penalty crime, while in a place where life is cheap, murder might be something you pay a small fine for.)" — [Wrede, Social Organization (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-social-organization/)
- "Are people guilty until proven innocent, innocent until proven guilty, or does it depend on the mood the lord is in when they bring the case before him?" — [Wrede, Social Organization (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-social-organization/)
- "What are considered normal and legal ways of gathering evidence and determining guilt? Is torture allowed?" — [Wrede, Social Organization (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-social-organization/)
- "Who is considered a citizen, with the rights and privileges thereof? What are those rights and privileges … and what responsibilities go along with them (jury duty, providing funds or knights for the lord's army?" — [Wrede, Social Organization (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-social-organization/)
- "Does the level of technological advancement match the level of social and political advancement?" — [Wrede, Social Organization (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-social-organization/)

### Economy / commerce
- "Is this a money-based economy, or mostly barter?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "Is currency and coinage standardized, or is there a system of exchange? What are typical denominations? … What are different coins called, and what are they worth?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "How is business organized? Are there trade unions? Guild structures? Multi-state corporations? International cartels?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "What is the process a young person goes through to enter a craft or trade? An apprenticeship? Four years of college?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "What industries — mining, fishing, shipbuilding, lumber, farming, manufacturing, etc. — are important, and in which areas? … What happens if supplies are disrupted?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "How much smuggling is there? Of what, from or to where?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)

### Technology (Wrede's "tech level" block — directly relevant to Source 7's pattern)
- "Is the level of technology in this society comparable to that of ancient Rome, the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, the Industrial Revolution, what?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "What important inventions or advances have been made (the wheel, gunpowder, printing, flush toilets)? Have any of them reached the point of affecting the daily life of the average person, or are flush toilets a luxury for the nobility only?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "What inventions or advances have *not* been made that you would normally expect to see at this stage of technological development? Which ones are about to be made?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "In what areas might magic replace technology, and thus suppress its development…? In what areas might magic cause more rapid technological or scientific development…?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)

### Transportation & communication
- "What is the fastest means of traveling long distances over land? Over water? Which methods are safest?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "How are messages sent when necessary? Is there a public/private postal system, or does everyone of importance have to send messengers? How fast can news get from A to B?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "How do people find out what is happening in the world — rumor, town crier, newspapers, TV and radio? How slanted is the news they get this way, and in what direction? Is there freedom of the press?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "Where would a traveler stay at night? Are there enough travelers to support inns, or do people have to stay at some equivalent of medieval monasteries?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)

### Medicine / healthcare
- "What level is medicine at? Who are the healers? Do you have to have a talent to heal, or just training?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "How expensive is a healer? How available are such services to ordinary people?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "How accurate is the diagnostic process? Do healers have ways of telling two diseases with similar symptoms apart?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "Is there a reliable method of birth control? Who normally handles births — midwives or doctors? What is the mortality rate for pregnant ladies, new mothers, and children?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "How is insanity treated? Are there asylums or treatment centers, or is it handled case-by-case?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)

### Arts, entertainment, leisure ("what do they do when bored")
- "What do people at various levels of society do for fun?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "What sports or pastimes are common (hawking, hunting, skiing, baseball)? Which ones take skill, money, and/or leisure time?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "What games are commonly known — chess, dice, poker? Which are common among everyone, and which are limited to the peasantry or nobility?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)
- "What is the status of the arts (dance, music, theater, etc.) in this society? Are artists revered or mistrusted?" — [Wrede, Commerce, Trade and Public Life (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/)

### Culture, customs, manners
- "When meeting someone, how are they greeted — wave, handshake, bow, some other gesture? How did the greeting gesture originate (example: shaking hands to prove one's weapon hand was empty)?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "Is there a way of changing a greeting gesture to make it insulting?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "What are the rites of passage in this culture? Are they formalized rituals, such as being dubbed a knight, or are they informal? Are they different for men and women? For nobility and peasants?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "What customs surround death and burial? … What things must be done and why (burn hair to free spirit, burn body to prevent necromancy, coins on eyes for ferryman, etc.)? Are the dead feared, revered, or ignored?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "What is a comfortable and polite speaking distance for people in this culture? Other cultures/countries/races? How aware are people of these differences?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "What are the social taboos — what things are 'not done,' like wearing a bathing suit to the office? What things are not talked about? What would happen if someone did?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "What things are considered normal and acceptable in this society that would not be considered normal or acceptable in yours?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "What will people swear a binding oath by? What do people use as curse words?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)

### Language / voice (useful for the Character workspace's "voice" category)
- "What are the variations in speech patterns, syntax, and slang from one social class to another? One occupation to another? One region to another? One race to another?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "What areas do local slang phrases come out of? (Example: in a fishing town, referring to good luck as 'a good catch,' while people in a farming town refer to it as 'an unexpected harvest'.)" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "What things in this culture would their language have many specific words for…? What do the people in this culture consider important enough to name? What does this say about the way they look at the world?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "What things would the people of this culture not have a name for, or have one word that covers numerous variations? What do they consider too unimportant to name?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)
- "Are there words that must never be spoken except at particular times, in ceremonies, or under particular circumstances?" — [Wrede, Peoples and Customs (SFWA)](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-peoples-and-customs/)

### Daily life (from Wrede's own site, 63 numbered questions)
- "What are accepted norms of personal hygiene? Do most people bathe regularly, or is bathing considered a health hazard?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "How is garbage and other waste material disposed of?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "What are the plumbing and sanitary systems like? Who builds and maintains them? How reliable are they, and who do you call when the drains back up? How do they differ from city to farm?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "How early do people get up in the morning in the city? Country? Are clocks common, or do people tell time by the sun or by listening for church bells?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "What items or foods or materials are luxuries — chocolate, coffee, silk, spices, flush toilets? Why?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "How many changes of clothes can a normal person afford? A noble person? A peasant?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "Are there sumptuary laws, defining who can wear what? What are the penalties?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "How do people cope with various disasters — fire, floods, tornadoes or hurricanes, blizzards, plague, etc.? How common are such disasters?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "Given the magical/technological level of this society, what is an appropriate ratio of farmers or food producers to urban residents? If farm production is based on magic, how many urban residents are going to starve if the spells supporting farming (weather, land fertility, etc.) fail suddenly?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "Given the state of roads and transportation, how much food is it possible to ship to a given location before it spoils? (This limits the size of cities.)" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "What is the level of literacy in the general population? … How common are books? How are they produced?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "How is the day divided into smaller time units? What are they (Hour of the Lark, Sunrise Bell, Nones, etc.)? … Is the length of an hour fixed, or does it vary…?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)
- "Are there any days that are considered 'outside the year,' like Mardi Gras or the Feast of Fools? How are these days customarily observed? How did they originate?" — [Wrede, Daily Life (pcwrede.com)](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/)

## 2. Chaotic Shiny (chaoticshiny.com)

**Framework description (verbatim):** "Chaotic Shiny is a generator site aimed at people who write, game, or live in fantasy worlds of their own creation." It is aimed at users "Grappling with writer's block", needing "a character on the fly", making a tavern more exciting, or fleshing out a setting with "some detailed religions." "Generators are divided into rough categories based on what they come up with." "Some of them, such as the drink and name generators, are simple, whereas others, such as the civilization and pantheon gens, are much more detailed." — [Chaotic Shiny homepage](http://chaoticshiny.com/)

Copyright line: "© Hannah 'Swordgleam' Lipsky 2010-2026" — [Chaotic Shiny Law Generator](http://chaoticshiny.com/lawgen.php)

**Important structural note for Lit Technica:** Chaotic Shiny is *not* a question bank. It is a randomiser. What is reusable is its **field taxonomy** — the slots it decides a civilization needs to have filled. Those field labels convert cleanly into prompts (e.g. field "Major Taboo" → "What is this civilization's major taboo?"). Below are the actual field labels, verbatim.

## 3. SFWA (sfwa.org) — worldbuilding craft resources

SFWA hosts both the Wrede reprint (Source 1) and its own tagged WORLDBUILDING article series. Three articles were fetched.

### 3a. "A Worldbuilding Guide to Crafting Diverse Cultures" — Amelia Wiens (31 Jan 2020)

**Description:** A question set for inventing fictional societies, explicitly "based on Trompenaars' model of cultural dimensions." Framing verbatim: "They're meant to be food for thought, so don't feel pressure to dive deep into every question. Get inspired and use what's useful to you." — [SFWA, Crafting Diverse Cultures](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/). This is the single best SFWA-native prompt list, and its opt-in framing again matches Lit Technica's dismissible-prompt model.

**Individualism vs. Communitarianism**
- "Do people in the society prefer to be unique or similar to each other?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "Does the culture value self-expression or conformity?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "Does the society value expressing personal opinions or maintaining harmony?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "Does the society value independent decision making and following your heart, or does the society value listening to advice and following the expectations of others?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "Does the culture value self-reliance or dependency on others?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)

**Universalism vs. Particularism**
- "What are the society's key moral principles?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "Do they apply these values consistently across contexts or does the application of these values vary depending on the situation and the relationships at hand? For instance, if you witness someone commit a crime, does your moral responsibility to report them change if the person is a stranger, your friend, or your boss?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)

**Neutral vs. Emotional**
- "To what extent does the society view emotions positively or negatively?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "Do people in the society hide their emotions or express them openly?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "When do people smile and what does smiling communicate in this society? Warmth and welcome in every context? Or perhaps lightheartedness and, in formal contexts, an inappropriate lack of seriousness?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)

**Specific vs. Diffuse**
- "How sophisticated and complex are the society's formal social conventions?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "What amounts of touch are socially acceptable in public? Holding hands? Hugging? Kissing?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)

**Achievement vs. Ascription**
- "In this society, where does status come from: an individual's achievements (their grades, discoveries and innovations, social activism, etc.) or the composition of their identity (their origin, age, gender, job title, etc.)?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "How strongly does the culture emphasize social hierarchy? What determines a person's place in the hierarchy?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)

**Sequential vs. Synchronous Time**
- "Does the society treat time linearly and set a specific time and order for completing tasks, or does the society treat time more fluidly and multi-task?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "What does the concept 'soon' mean within the society? Within an hour, day, week, month, year?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)
- "What does it mean to be late in this society? Arriving one minute, five minutes, thirty minutes, an hour, or multiple hours after the specified time? At what point will others get annoyed by tardiness?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/)

### 3b. "The Ecology of Worldbuilding" — Jendia Gammon (25 Oct 2022)

**Description:** Applies ecological science (biotic/abiotic factors, food chains, apex predators) to genre worldbuilding. Verbatim: "By applying principles of ecology to genre (or any) fiction, you can make your world(s) more believable, anchoring even the most fantastical settings with realism." — [SFWA, Ecology of Worldbuilding](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/)

- "Where does each creature in your world fit in a food chain?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/)
- "What happens when you take the top predator away?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/)
- "What sorts of population pressures do your characters face?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/)
- "What biotic and abiotic factors affect them?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/)
- "Is your dragon the apex predator in the food chain, or is something else the apex predator?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/)
- "Does a special herb necessary for medicine only exist on the leeward side of particular stones?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/)
- "Have you accounted for all the ways your creatures, characters, and other living things will affect one another in your story world?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/)
- "Can they breathe?" / "What are their vulnerabilities?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/)
- Consistency check: "Your fantasy characters must eat, grow crops, find shelter, and otherwise provide for their basic needs within your created world." — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/)

### 3c. "The Art of Story as Worldbuilding" — Nathan Nance (16 Oct 2019)

**Description:** A restraint-oriented counterweight — argues worldbuilding should surface through character, not exposition. Useful as *pitfall checks* rather than generative prompts.

- "Don't explain it unless you must." — [SFWA, Art of Story as Worldbuilding](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/)
- "Show the reader your world through your character, because worldbuilding is a triple frontier—plot, character, and world intersect in countless ways." — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/)
- "Too much bores the reader. Too little makes the story shallow." — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/)
- "Tell me about the culture and the art and the magic when it's necessary to the plot and the character." — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/)
- "The danger with the Worldbuilding First approach is one of getting bogged down and never finding out who your characters are through *experimentation.*" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/)
- Character-facing diagnostic: "Is she in danger of death because of the type of magic she uses?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/)
- "Does the ship's engineer have a mechanical arm, and does that make him feel disabled?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/)
- "If the mechanical arm has a welding torch, does he think of it as a superpower or does he long for flesh and blood?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/)
- Limits check: "Why didn't he know that the rogue intended to steal his crystal ball worth ten thousand ducats?" — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/)
- "In writing, character affects plot. Plot affects worldbuilding. And worldbuilding affects character." — [SFWA](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/)

## 4. TV Tropes — worldbuilding craft advice and pitfalls

**Description:** TV Tropes' "World Building" page defines the practice and catalogues its failure modes; "Info Dump" and "As You Know" are the two canonical exposition-failure pages. These are best used in Lit Technica as *checks* ("is this an infodump?") rather than generative prompts.

### Framing / definition
- Epigraph (Umberto Eco, postscript to *The Name of the Rose*, quoted on the page): "It is necessary to create constraints, in order to invent freely… In fiction, the surrounding world provides the constraint. This has nothing to do with realism… A completely unreal world can be constructed, in which asses fly and princesses are restored to life by a kiss; but that world, purely possible and unrealistic, must exist according to structures defined at the outset…" — [TV Tropes, World Building](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WorldBuilding)
- Worldbuilding is "the process of constructing a fictional universe." — [TV Tropes, World Building](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WorldBuilding)
- Sense 1: "The creation of a Fantasy World Map, history, geography, ecology, mythology, Technology Levels, several different cultures in detail, and usually a set of 'ground rules', metaphysical or otherwise." — [TV Tropes, World Building](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WorldBuilding)
- Sense 2: "The work that goes into deciding the details of a setting." — [TV Tropes, World Building](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WorldBuilding)
- On implied depth: "the portion of a story which becomes known over time without ever actually being directly described." — [TV Tropes, World Building](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WorldBuilding)

### Pitfalls, usable as checks
- Excess detail not relevant to the story is identified as "lore porn"; C. S. Lewis was criticised by Tolkien for making Narnia a "Fantasy Kitchen Sink" world. — [TV Tropes, World Building](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WorldBuilding)
- Lovingly crafted novels "often have… a slow beginning." — [TV Tropes, World Building](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WorldBuilding)
- A Universe Bible often specifies what may and may not be done — e.g. "The original *Star Trek* series bible explicitly forbidding the Enterprise from visiting 23rd-century Earth." — [TV Tropes, World Building](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WorldBuilding)
- The page itself points writers to Wrede's World Building Questions and to "Mark Rosenfelder's Language Construction Kit." — [TV Tropes, World Building](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WorldBuilding)

### Info Dump
- "Infodumping is a type of Exposition that is particularly long or wordy." — [TV Tropes, Info Dump](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/InfoDump)
- "Most of the time, however, infodumps are obvious, intrusive, patronizing, and sometimes downright boring because they don't advance the plot at all, especially if they have no visual aids." — [TV Tropes, Info Dump](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/InfoDump)
- "The absolute worst is the gratuitous infodump, which painfully restates that which has already been adequately shown, just to make the audience suffer." — [TV Tropes, Info Dump](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/InfoDump)
- Workaround named on the page: having one character be "The Watson," because that character's presence creates a reason for others to explain things. — [TV Tropes, Info Dump](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/InfoDump)

### As You Know
- "This is a form of exposition where one character explains to another something that they both know, but which the audience doesn't or may have forgotten." — [TV Tropes, As You Know](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AsYouKnow)
- Canonical parody line: "As you know, Alice, my death ray depends on codfish balls." — [TV Tropes, As You Know](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AsYouKnow)
- Also called "As You Know, Bob", abbreviated "AYKB"; the fantasy version per Terry Pratchett is the "As you know, your father, the king..." speech. — [TV Tropes, As You Know](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AsYouKnow)
- Name-drop tell: "Say, Alice, how are you enjoying your coffee?" / "Why, it's delicious, Bob, thanks for asking. How are you coming along, Charlie?" — [TV Tropes, As You Know](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AsYouKnow)
- Fixes listed: "Writers try to avoid this by using The Watson, and thus the most common alternative is to give the protagonist amnesia so he *doesn't* know, which isn't really considered a better option." Others: The Idiot Hero, Fish out of Water, or having characters argue, because "arguments are among the few real-life situations in which people remind each other of things they both already know." — [TV Tropes, As You Know](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AsYouKnow)
- When it is defensible: "Not explaining anything sometimes results in the audience being too busy trying to figure out what's going on to enjoy the show, using this trope is not always a bad thing." — [TV Tropes, As You Know](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AsYouKnow)

## 5. Orson Scott Card — the MICE quotient

**Verification note:** Card's own books (*Characters & Viewpoint*; *How to Write Science Fiction and Fantasy*) were not fetchable as primary text in this session. Card's original page on hatrack.com returned a client error. Below I use two well-attributed secondary sources: one that quotes Card's book verbatim with page numbers, and one podcast transcript that lays out the begin/end logic.

**Framework description (verbatim from the Writing Excuses transcript):** "This is a theory that Orson Scott Card talks about in two of his books, Characters & Viewpoint and How to Write Science Fiction and Fantasy. The idea is… MICE stands for milieu, idea, character, and event. The idea is that every story is made up of those four elements. And that at any given point, one of those elements may dominate. In short stories, you may choose only one of those elements. In novels, you usually have all four playing against each other." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)

**Card's own definitions, quoted with page cites from *Character and Viewpoint*:**
- Milieu — "landscape, the interior spaces, the surrounding cultures . . . everything from weather to traffic laws." (Card, p. 62) — [Nate Listrom](https://www.natelistrom.com/2022/11/13/mice-internal-external.html)
- Idea — "the information that the reader is meant to discover or learn during the process of the story." (Card, p. 62) — [Nate Listrom](https://www.natelistrom.com/2022/11/13/mice-internal-external.html)
- Character — "the nature of [a person] in the story — what they do and why." (Card, p. 63) — [Nate Listrom](https://www.natelistrom.com/2022/11/13/mice-internal-external.html)
- Event — "everything that *happens* and why." (Card, p. 63) — [Nate Listrom](https://www.natelistrom.com/2022/11/13/mice-internal-external.html)

### Begin/end symmetry (the actual diagnostic engine)
- "Where you start and where you end mirror each other." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- Milieu: "A milieu story starts when you enter the space" and "ends when you exit it." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- Idea: "Idea stories are stories in which you start with a question." It "ends when you answer the question." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- Character: stories "start with a character who is dissatisfied with their lot in life or some aspect of their life" and end "when they either become satisfied or become reconciled to the fact that they're just stuck with this." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- Event: "Event stories are things in which something goes horribly, horribly wrong with the status quo." They "either end with the solution or when everybody dies." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)

### Diagnostic prompts this framework implies (all verbatim from the transcript)
- "So what you want to look at with the MICE quotient is where do you start the story and where do you end it? That will also tell you probably who your point of view character is." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- "What is a chapter, where you end?" — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- "If you say this is an idea chapter, a character walks on stage wanting an answer to this question, that is part of the overarching plot. Therefore we will end the chapter when the answer is either given or there is no answer to be found." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- Promise test: "The whole concept here is the fulfilling of promises." / "You're making a compact with your reader at the beginning of the book about what sort of story to expect." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- Mismatch failure, in Card's own example as relayed: "the detective who is investigating, working with the widow of the man, gradually falls in love, and they get married and live happily ever after. You never find out what happened about the body. You will hate that author so much." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- Opening-line diagnostic: "If the book begins, 'There was a body on the floor and it was my job to figure out who put it there,' then we know we've got a detective story." vs. "If the book begins, 'There was a beautiful woman in the room, and she was distracting me from the fact that there was also a body on the floor,' then we might be able to actually come to grips with that story being a romance in which the body isn't the problem and the woman is." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- Nesting rule: "Make promises and fulfill them. These can be nested, but close them in the order you open them. (Actually, reverse order — MI … IM)." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- Scope: "The MICE framework can be used at multiple levels, story, chapter, scene." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)
- Exercise: "Red Riding Hood. That's a great one. MICE quotient for Red Riding Hood. Try and write a page of each story of the different things for MICE." — [Writing Excuses 6.10](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/)

**Variant worth noting:** some later treatments rename Idea → Inquiry. "Milieu stories open with arrival in a place and close on departure." / "Inquiry stories open with a question and close when it is answered." / "Character stories open with discontent and close when identity changes." / "Event stories open with disruption and close when order is restored or replaced." — [Elements of Story, MICE Quotient](https://elementsofstory.com/entries/mice-quotient)

## 7. Technology-level / society-consistency checklists

Two sources match the brief's "for each area of daily life, ask what the most advanced available option is" pattern. Wrede's Science & Technology block (Section 1 above) is a third.

### 7a. The Weltenbau Wissen List of Worldbuilding Questions (Michael Waning; English trans. 2017)

**Description (verbatim):** "The Weltenbau Wissen List of Worldbuilding Questions offers a step-by-step approach to develop a setting." / "The list is meant for all genres and media – every worldbuilder must decide for themselves which questions they want to answer for their worlds. For a world to be consistent and vibrant not all questions need to be answered – nor do I claim the list to be complete." — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)

Its consistency argument, verbatim: "The challenge all worldbuilders face is making their fictional world believable and giving it the illusion of largeness… This only works with a combination of both creativity and consistency: clichés and inconsistencies will destroy the immersion." — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)

Structure: I. Cosmology and Metaphysics (1–8); II. Geography and climate (9–19); III. Ecology and biology (20–32); IV. Cultures and history (33–41); V. Society and religion (42–66) — Social structures 42–47, Values and order 48–55, Economy and education 56–61, Religion 62–66; VI. Technology and magic (67–82) — Technology 67–74, Magic 75–82. — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)

**Technology (Q67–74) — this is the exact "most advanced available option" pattern**
- Q67: "What is the highest technology level in the largest mainstream civilisation? Which raw materials and energy sources do they use and how safe are they? How advanced is science?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q68: "How wide is the gap in development between the technologically most and least advanced societies, or the most privileged and poorest classes within a single society?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q69: "How is knowledge recorded and passed on? Are literacy and a basic understanding of math widespread?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q70: "Is some work typically done by hand? Do machines or computers help?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q71 (communication): "What means of communication are there, which are particularly progressive, which ones are commonplace and widely available?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q72 (transport): "What means of transport exist, which are particularly progressive, which ones are commonplace and widely available? What distances can be travelled safely, what would be a risky expedition or thought of as impossible – e.g. sailing around the world, travel to other galaxies?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q73 (healthcare): "How far has medicine progressed, which diseases and injuries can be treated successfully?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q74 (warfare): "What kind of technologies are used for armed conflicts?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q82 (magic/tech interaction): "What is the relationship between magic and technology? Are there – thanks to magic – otherwise impossible achievements in material processing, communication, transport, warfare, medicine or architecture?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)

**Daily life / society consistency**
- Q52: "Which rights and/or duties does an average individual in society have? What about freedom of movement, the right to choose their own life's path?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q53: "Are there any customs or traits that are typical for the society, and how do these represent the societal values and/or the societal/economical standing? Think of clothing, festivals and celebrations, food, greetings, architecture." — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q57: "What is the standard of living? Is there a concentration of wealth of resources, owned by a few, or is the wealth spread out evenly?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q58: "How are goods and wares produced and how does one accumulate wealth? Is there a trading economy, currencies, credit? Is there an open market?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q59: "Is education important? How does one acquire an education? Are there rules concerning access to education? How is knowledge of crafts, magic and/or technology passed on?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q61 (leisure/arts): "How important is art (music, literature, dance, acting, sculpting, painting etc.) in all ranks of society? Is there funding? Are artists respected?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)
- Q62: "How important is religion? Is it the core of societal values and daily life, or is it observed privately?" — [Weltenbau Wissen PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf)

### 7b. Write On Sisters — "1 Key Question for Worldbuilding: A Handy Checklist" (Heather Jackson)

**Description:** Six categories — History, Community, Leadership, Economy, Education, Infrastructure — each run through the "good old 5 W's" (WHO / WHAT / WHERE / WHEN / WHY), with one overriding question. The central question, verbatim: "*WHY are things like this?*" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)

Its rationale, verbatim: "Writers can come up with all kinds of cool stuff for their made-up worlds, but if there is no *reasoning* behind that stuff, if they haven't asked *why* and come up with answers, they may end up with a world that at best feels underdeveloped and at worst doesn't make sense. So every time you build something into your fictional world, ask why it's there and why it's like that." — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)

**Infrastructure** — defined as covering "technology, transportation, communication, food production, water supply, waste disposal, health, law" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- Technology: "What kind of technology does this world have? Are they still working on the wheel, or have they perfected space travel?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- Transport: "How do people get around? Cars? Horses? Foot? Transit? Magic carpet?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- Communication: "How do people communicate? What media (if any) is available to spread news and entertainment?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- Healthcare: "Is there health care? What happens if someone gets sick or injured? Do they go to the hospital or call the local shaman?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- Law: "Are there laws? How are they enforced? Is there an established legal system? Are there law enforcement officers?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- Water: "How will you get safe, drinkable water?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- Waste: "What will you do with human waste now that the sewer system no longer works?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- Food: "How will you get food once the grocery stores are raided and stripped bare?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)

**Community**
- "WHO inhabits this world? Are they all human or not? Think about race, social classes, gender, etc. Who gets along? Who causes trouble? Who are allies and who are enemies? Does everyone speak the same language or not?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- "WHAT roles do people play in society? What are the social rituals? What traditions do people adhere to regarding birth, marriage, child-rearing and death?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- "WHEN was this community and its rituals established? How ingrained are the traditions and rituals? Does anyone remember how things used to be?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)

**Economy**
- "WHO works? Who doesn't work? Are there employers and employees, or is this a society where everyone chips in equally?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- "WHAT are the jobs in this world? What is the payment: money (paper, coins, gold bars) or a barter system? Do people make enough to acquire personal possessions, or are they barely putting food on the table?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- "WHY do people work? Because they want to or because they have to?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)

**Leadership**
- "WHO is in charge? Is there a system of government? Is there religion? Is there a community leader?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- "WHERE are the leaders? Living amongst the commoners or high in an ivory castle?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- "WHY are they the leaders? Were they born into the position, or elected, or did they take power by force?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)

**Education**
- "WHO is educated? Everyone or only certain classes/races/genders?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- "WHAT types of education are available? Formal schooling? Apprenticeships? A smart chip in one's brain?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)

**History**
- "WHAT happened to cause the Main Problem?" / "WHERE did it happen? Everywhere? Or were only certain places in the world affected?" — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)
- "WHY did the world become like this? This is very different than 'what happened' … When answering WHY, focus on character response." — [Write On Sisters](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/)

## 8. Escalation / stakes-testing prompts for scenes and plot

Four sources verified. Robert McKee supplies the canonical "worst thing" test; Savannah Gilbo supplies a step-by-step scene diagnostic; Jami Gold supplies the definition and the "does it have to rise every scene?" nuance; K.M. Weiland's list exercise is captured via a blog that describes it.

### 8a. Robert McKee, *Story* — the risk test and progressive complications

**The canonical prompt, verbatim:** "Here's a simple test to apply to any story. Ask: What is the risk? What does the protagonist stand to lose if he does not get what he wants? More specifically, what's the worst thing that will happen to the protagonist if he does not achieve his desire?" — [McKee, *Story* (PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5af1145d697a987d247c2773/t/5f134b8555e4ba6ef215f38a/1595100062763/Story-+Style,+Structure,+Substance,+and+the+Principles+of+Screenwriting+by+Robert+McKee.pdf)

**The failure signal, verbatim:** "If this question cannot be answered in a compelling way, the story is misconceived at its core. For example, if the answer is: 'Should the protagonist fail, life would go back to normal,' this story is not worth telling." — [McKee, *Story* (PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5af1145d697a987d247c2773/t/5f134b8555e4ba6ef215f38a/1595100062763/Story-+Style,+Structure,+Substance,+and+the+Principles+of+Screenwriting+by+Robert+McKee.pdf)

**Escalation mechanic (progressive complications), verbatim:**
- "The protagonist's first action has aroused forces of antagonism that block his desire and spring open a gap between anticipation and result… putting him in greater conflict with his world, at even greater risk." — [McKee, *Story* (PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5af1145d697a987d247c2773/t/5f134b8555e4ba6ef215f38a/1595100062763/Story-+Style,+Structure,+Substance,+and+the+Principles+of+Screenwriting+by+Robert+McKee.pdf)
- "Over and over again in a progression, rather than cooperation, his actions provoke forces of antagonism, opening gaps in his reality. This pattern repeats on various levels to the end of the line, to a final action beyond which the audience cannot imagine another." — [McKee, *Story* (PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5af1145d697a987d247c2773/t/5f134b8555e4ba6ef215f38a/1595100062763/Story-+Style,+Structure,+Substance,+and+the+Principles+of+Screenwriting+by+Robert+McKee.pdf)
- The action/activity distinction — a direct scene test: "True action is physical, vocal, or mental movement that opens gaps in expectation and creates significant change. Mere activity is behavior in which what is expected happens, generating either no change or trivial change." — [McKee, *Story* (PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5af1145d697a987d247c2773/t/5f134b8555e4ba6ef215f38a/1595100062763/Story-+Style,+Structure,+Substance,+and+the+Principles+of+Screenwriting+by+Robert+McKee.pdf)
- "The substance of story is the gap that splits open between what a human being expects to happen when he takes an action and what really does happen… To build a scene, we constantly break open these breaches in reality." — [McKee, *Story* (PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5af1145d697a987d247c2773/t/5f134b8555e4ba6ef215f38a/1595100062763/Story-+Style,+Structure,+Substance,+and+the+Principles+of+Screenwriting+by+Robert+McKee.pdf)
- Scene definition (usable as a "did this scene turn?" check): "A scene is an action through conflict in more or less continuous time and space that turns the value-charged condition of a character's life on at least one value with a degree of perceptible significance. Ideally, every scene is a STORY EVENT." — [McKee, *Story* (PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5af1145d697a987d247c2773/t/5f134b8555e4ba6ef215f38a/1595100062763/Story-+Style,+Structure,+Substance,+and+the+Principles+of+Screenwriting+by+Robert+McKee.pdf)
- Value law: "The measure of the value of a character's desire is in direct proportion to the risk he's willing to take to achieve it; the greater the value, the greater the risk." — [McKee, *Story* (PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5af1145d697a987d247c2773/t/5f134b8555e4ba6ef215f38a/1595100062763/Story-+Style,+Structure,+Substance,+and+the+Principles+of+Screenwriting+by+Robert+McKee.pdf)

*Note: the phrase "law of conflict" does not appear in the fetched text of this PDF.*

### 8b. Savannah Gilbo — "Progressive Complications" (a five-step scene diagnostic)

This is the closest published match to Lit Technica's "if this scene continued, what would change?" prompt idea, since it explicitly asks writers to rank and re-order complications in a draft scene.

- Step 1: "The first thing you'll want to do is identify the point of view character's goal in the scene." / "So, what does your character want to achieve, accomplish, or get? And then, what's their plan for achieving, accomplishing, or getting their goal?" — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)
- "The next thing you'll want to think of in terms of your character's goal is -- What do they expect to happen when they act on their plan?" — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)
- Step 2: "make a chronological list of everything that gets in your character's way." — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)
- Step 3 (ranking): "You can rank the complications from 1-10 (1 being the least complicated and 10 being the most complicated)." — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)
- The escalation test, verbatim: "The most important part of this step is to determine whether or not your complications escalate." / "So, do they get worse over time? Can you rank them like 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6… or do they look more like 1, 3, 3, 2, 5, 1…?" — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)
- "If you find yourself with complications that jump all over the place (like from 1 to 3 to 10 to 1), this is a clear indicator that you need to revise your scene." — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)
- Step 4 (turning point): "the turning point is a moment where your character realizes their original plan for accomplishing their scene goal is no longer viable." / "So, think about it like a moment where your character says, 'Well NOW what am I going to do?'" — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)
- "If you *have* identified a turning point, the next thing you'll want to do is ask yourself how that turning point changes things." / "So, what's changed in this scene? And if there is something that's changed, how do you feel about that change? Does it matter?" — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)
- Kill test: "If there isn't a turning point that changes things and forces your character to make a decision, then your scene doesn't work and it's time to make some tweaks." — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)
- Global check: "Does it move the global story forward? Where does this one scene fall within the chain of conflict (or the chain of progressive complications) for your global story? How does this scene help create that arc of change in your global story?" — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)
- Rising-tension rule: "the complications in your story should be presented in an escalating fashion like—1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6—not 1, 2, 3, 3, 5, 1." — [Savannah Gilbo](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications)

### 8c. Jami Gold (Writers Helping Writers) — "What Does It Mean To 'Raise the Stakes'?"

- Definition, verbatim: "Stakes are simply **the consequences of failure**. If our character doesn't reach their goal, what will happen? What can go wrong?" — [Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2017/03/what-does-it-mean-to-raise-the-stakes/)
- "Without consequences for failure, readers have no reason to care about or root for a certain outcome—any will do." — [Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2017/03/what-does-it-mean-to-raise-the-stakes/)
- Calibration prompts: "Ever wonder what counts as *increasing stakes*? Are stakes less than life-and-death too weak? Or if the protagonist *is* at risk of death, how do we increase the stakes from there?" — [Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2017/03/what-does-it-mean-to-raise-the-stakes/)
- Reframe: "'Raising the stakes' refers to how close the cost hits to home for *that* character. How much would failure 'attack' their sense of self, who they are or want to be?" — [Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2017/03/what-does-it-mean-to-raise-the-stakes/)
- Important nuance for a non-nagging prompt system: "Stakes don't have to increase *every* scene. Some scenes can *reinforce* stakes, reminding readers of the risks. Or scenes can *deepen* stakes, with the character becoming more involved with the same risks." — [Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2017/03/what-does-it-mean-to-raise-the-stakes/)
- Scene-level goal requirement: "scenes should also have **a specific, immediate goal**." — [Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2017/03/what-does-it-mean-to-raise-the-stakes/)
- Subplot tactic: "Subplots are often a good place to *let our characters fail completely* with no opportunity to 'fix' the situation." — [Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2017/03/what-does-it-mean-to-raise-the-stakes/)
- Closing prompt: "How do you raise the stakes in your scenes?" — [Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2017/03/what-does-it-mean-to-raise-the-stakes/)

### 8d. K.M. Weiland's "ten worst things" exercise (as described by One Wild Word)

## Cross-source observations for Lit Technica

1. **The dismissible-prompt model has strong precedent.** Wrede ("If it's useful, use it. If not, don't."), Wiens ("don't feel pressure to dive deep into every question"), and Waning ("not all questions need to be answered") all explicitly disclaim completeness. Citing those framings in-product would be both accurate and reassuring to writers.
2. **Two distinct prompt grammars exist.** World prompts are third-person about the setting ("What is the level of technology…"); character prompts are frequently second-person addressed *to the character* ("What is your greatest fear?" — Proust) or second-person addressed to the *writer* about the character ("What is in your character's refrigerator right now?" — Gotham). Lit Technica should pick deliberately per workspace.
3. **The "most advanced available option" pattern is real and citable**, best evidenced by Weltenbau Wissen Q71–Q73 ("which are particularly progressive, which ones are commonplace and widely available?") and Wrede's "Have any of them reached the point of affecting the daily life of the average person, or are flush toilets a luxury for the nobility only?"
4. **Pitfall checks should ship alongside generative prompts.** TV Tropes' Info Dump / As You Know pages and SFWA's Nance article all warn that worldbuilding depth is a liability when surfaced badly — a natural fit for a second prompt class ("checks") distinct from "questions."
5. **Escalation prompts have a canonical wording to anchor on:** McKee's "what's the worst thing that will happen to the protagonist if he does not achieve his desire?" plus Gilbo's rank-and-reorder scene test.
