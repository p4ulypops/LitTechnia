# TTRPG Prompt / Oracle / Random-Table Mechanics for Worldbuilding, Character & Plot

**Research brief:** structured prompts, oracles, random tables and gamified mechanics used to build fictional worlds, settings and characters — collaboratively or solo. Compiled as source material for a craft-guidance content library in the Lit Technica novel-writing app (optional, attributed, non-mandatory prompts in Worldbuilding / Character / Plot / Timeline workspaces).

**Grounding note:** every quotation below was extracted from a page fetched during this research session. Where an official publisher page contained only marketing copy, a mirror or credible secondary source was used and is explicitly flagged as non-primary. Failed/blocked fetches are listed at the end.

**Attribution note for product use:** all quoted text below is copyrighted by its respective authors/publishers. It is reproduced here as research evidence. Any in-app feature should paraphrase the *mechanic* and credit the source by name; it should not reproduce prompt wording verbatim.

## 1. Microscope (Ben Robbins / Lame Mage Productions)

### What it is
A GM-less, prep-less collaborative history-building game for 2–4 players. The official site describes it as: "A role-playing game for two to four players. No GM. No prep. Microscope was playtested for two years by over 150 awesome gamers." ([Lame Mage — Microscope](https://www.lamemage.com/microscope/)). Its three taglines are "Mock chronological order.", "Defy time and space.", and "Build worlds and destroy them." ([Lame Mage — Microscope](https://www.lamemage.com/microscope/)).

Example premises given on the official page: "Humanity spreads to the stars and forges a galactic civilization…", "Fledgling nations arise from the ruins of the empire…", "An ancient line of dragon-kings dies out as magic fades from the realm…" ([Lame Mage — Microscope](https://www.lamemage.com/microscope/)).

The key structural claim: "You won't play the game in chronological order. You can defy the limits of time and space, jumping backward or forward to explore the parts of the history that interest you." ([Lame Mage — Microscope](https://www.lamemage.com/microscope/)).

### How the mechanic works
The official product page does **not** publish the rules text; the following procedural detail comes from a detailed public rules cheat sheet (secondary source, clearly labelled as such): ([Nicole van der Hoeven — Microscope (RPG) rules cheat sheet](https://notes.nicolevanderhoeven.com/Microscope+(RPG)+rules+cheat+sheet)).

1. **Craft an overview (Big Picture).** "Collaboratively decide on a one-sentence/phrase summary of the type of game you want to play, including the genre." Worked examples: "*Three nations are united as a single empire*", "*Refugees carve out a new life in a distant land*", "*A new force changes society, wiping away the old values*". Follow-up interrogation prompts: "What kind of nations are they? Feudal kingdoms, primitive tribes, modern superpowers, stellar clusters, or colonies on an alien world?" and "Do the people of these nations share the same culture? Are they even the same race?" ([cheat sheet](https://notes.nicolevanderhoeven.com/Microscope+(RPG)+rules+cheat+sheet))
2. **Bookend history.** "Create two periods (large chunks of time of decades or centuries each), a start and an end", each with a short description and a light/dark tone marker — "Decide whether each period is 'light' (add an empty circle to its card) or 'dark' (add a filled circle)." ([cheat sheet](https://notes.nicolevanderhoeven.com/Microscope+(RPG)+rules+cheat+sheet))
3. **Palette.** "Create two columns, 'Yes' and 'No', and take turns filling them out… Put something in 'Yes' if you think others wouldn't expect it to be in the game but you'd like to include it. Put something in 'No' if you think others *would* expect it to be in the game, but you'd like to exclude it." The cheat sheet adds: "This is the last time you're going to be actually building something by consensus." ([cheat sheet](https://notes.nicolevanderhoeven.com/Microscope+(RPG)+rules+cheat+sheet))
4. **Turn structure — the Lens.** Each round one player is the *Lens*: "Declare the focus… Make history… Lens finishes the Focus… Choose a new Legacy… Explore a legacy… New Lens." A Focus can be "a person / a place / a thing / an institution / an event / a period / a concept". Example given: "The new Focus is going to be the 'sinking of the Gabriel Dora.' It's a luxury liner that goes down mysteriously, so first I'm making a new Event where the ship sinks in the North Atlantic, with no known survivors…" ([cheat sheet](https://notes.nicolevanderhoeven.com/Microscope+(RPG)+rules+cheat+sheet))

Content nests three levels deep: Period → Event → Scene.

### Scene Questions (the most directly portable prompt mechanic)
Ben Robbins' own design blog specifies the rule for framing a scene, and it is unusually crisp:

> "When you make a Question for a scene, it must be about people. Like what someone does, says, or thinks. It could be one person ("Does the doctor regret her decision?", "Is the soldier remembered as a hero?") or a category of people ("Do the miners go on strike?", "Do the villagers believe the prophet?")" ([Ars Ludi — "A Microscope for the People"](https://arsludi.lamemage.com/index.php/3449/a-microscope-for-the-people/))

> "Questions must be phrased to have a yes or no answer ("Did the priest bless the crown?") or a similar simple binary choice." ([Ars Ludi](https://arsludi.lamemage.com/index.php/3449/a-microscope-for-the-people/))

> "When you're in a scene, just play your character. Describe what they do, say, and think. Don't introduce new facts about the world, just work with what you already know. If you need to introduce or clarify something about the world outside of your character, discuss it with the other players. It's only true if everyone else agrees." ([Ars Ludi](https://arsludi.lamemage.com/index.php/3449/a-microscope-for-the-people/))

Robbins' rationale is worth noting for design purposes: "With these changes, scenes are out of the business of narrating world-facts, and are purely focused on seeing what people do and think." And: "If the question is 'does the Captain believe in her mission?' and you're playing the Captain, everyone knows from the start of the scene that what you do is critical." ([Ars Ludi](https://arsludi.lamemage.com/index.php/3449/a-microscope-for-the-people/)). The same post retires an older mechanic: "Which also means the Push mechanic can go on the scrap heap. Rest in peace, Push!" ([Ars Ludi](https://arsludi.lamemage.com/index.php/3449/a-microscope-for-the-people/)).

### What makes it gamified
- Strict **turn order** with a rotating authority role (Lens) — no consensus after the Palette.
- A **binary tone token** (empty circle = light, filled circle = dark) applied to every Period/Event/Scene.
- A hard **nesting hierarchy** (Period > Event > Scene) that constrains where new content may be added.
- **Legacies** as a queue of elements that must be revisited.
- A **Yes/No Palette** that gates content in/out before play.
- Scene resolution requires a binary Question, converting freeform improvisation into an answerable prompt.

### Direct relevance to Lit Technica
The Timeline workspace maps almost 1:1 onto Microscope: bookend Periods, non-chronological infill, a light/dark tone toggle per entry, and a "Legacy" flag marking elements the writer has promised themselves to revisit. The Scene Question rule ("must be about people; must be yes/no") is an excellent, cheap, non-mandatory nudge for a Plot workspace.

**URLs:** https://www.lamemage.com/microscope/ ; https://arsludi.lamemage.com/index.php/3449/a-microscope-for-the-people/ ; https://notes.nicolevanderhoeven.com/Microscope+(RPG)+rules+cheat+sheet (secondary)

## 2. The Quiet Year (Avery Alder / Buried Without Ceremony)

**Source caveat:** the full ruleset and complete card list were extracted from a PDF mirror of the rulebook (`i.4pcdn.org`), not from the publisher's own site. It is the full text of the game, but it is a **non-primary mirror**. ([The Quiet Year rulebook PDF (mirror)](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

### What it is
A GM-less map-drawing game for a post-collapse community. Stated parameters: "**2-4 players** and **2-4 hours**"; "A full-length game tends to run **3-4 hours including teaching time**"; the community has "**60-80 members**, unless otherwise stated"; the game needs "six dice, 20 Contempt Tokens, a blank piece of letter-sized paper, pencils, erasers, an index card, a deck of The Quiet Year cards, and at least one copy of the summary card." ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

### How the mechanic works
A standard 52-card deck maps to 52 weeks, one suit per season: ♥ Spring, ♦ Summer, ♣ Autumn, ♠ Winter ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf)).

> "The basic unit of play in The Quiet Year is the week. Each week is a turn taken by one player, with play proceeding clockwise around the table. Weeks should take an average of 2-3 minutes to complete." ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

Each week: the active player draws a card and reads it aloud, resolving all **bold** text; project dice are reduced by 1; the player then takes exactly one of three actions.

**The three actions, verbatim:**

- *Discover Something New* — "Introduce a new situation. It might be a problem, an opportunity, or a bit of both. Draw that situation onto the map. Drawings should be small and simple: smaller than an inch, finished within thirty seconds." Example situations given: "There's a dried-up well located at the edge of town." / "Mangy wolves have been slinking around the woods." / "There's a broken-down waterwheel a mile upstream." / "Strange wailing noises come from the forest at night." / "A self-declared prophet arrives." ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))
- *Hold a Discussion* — "You can choose to open with a question or a declaration. Starting with you and going clockwise, everyone gets to weigh in once, sharing a single argument comprised of 1-2 sentences… A discussion never results in a decision or summation process. Everyone weighs in, and then it's over. This is how conversations work in communities: they are untidy and inconclusive affairs." Examples: "Should we retaliate against the bikers? (Or, if leading with a declaration: We should abstain from retaliation or violence.)" / "Could we use the school-bus as a sleeping area for the village children?" ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))
- *Start a Project* — "You choose a situation and declare what the community will do to resolve it. There is no consultation about this idea - the community simply begins work." Examples: "We're converting the mineshaft into a cold food storage." / "We're killing those wolves." / "We're going to sacrifice a newborn on the night of the full moon, to appease the Windwalkers." ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

**Project dice:** "When our community starts a project, we'll place one on the map to note how many weeks it will take to finish. Each week, they tick down by one. When a die reaches zero, the project is complete." Duration is "minimum 1 and maximum 6" weeks; "If a project would reasonably take longer than six weeks to complete, it will need to be completed in stages." ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

**Contempt tokens:** "They represent any tension and frustration that might arise in the community… If someone starts a project that you don't agree with, you don't get to voice your objections or speak out of turn. You are instead invited to take a piece of Contempt." They can be spent two ways: "If you ever want to act selfishly, to the known detriment of the community, you can discard a Contempt token to justify your behaviour" and "If someone else does something that you greatly support, that would mend relationships and rebuild trust, you can discard a Contempt token to demonstrate how they have diffused past tensions." ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

**Abundances / Scarcities:** an index card headed "Abundances, Scarcities, and Names". Example resources: "clean drinking water / a source of energy / protection from predators / adequate shelter / food". "Choosing a resource makes it important, if it wasn't already. If you pick 'gasoline,' it becomes something that your community wants and needs." ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

### Verbatim card prompts (selected across all four suits)
Each card offers two alternative prompts ("or…"), so the drawer chooses.

**Spring (♥)**
| Card | Prompt |
|---|---|
| A | "What group has the highest status in the community? What must people do to gain inclusion in this group?" / "Are there distinct family units in the community? If so, what family structures are common?" |
| 2 | "There's a large body of water on the map. Where is it? What does it look like?" / "There's a giant, man-made structure on the map. Where is it? Why is it abandoned?" |
| 4 | "What important and basic tools does the community lack?" / "Where are you storing your food? Why is this a risky place to store things?" |
| 5 | "There is a disquieting legend about this place. What is it?" / "Alarming weather patterns destroy something. How and what?" |
| 7 | "Where does everyone sleep? Who is unhappy with this arrangement, and why?" / "What natural predators roam this area? Are you safe?" |
| 8 | "An old piece of machinery is discovered, broken but perhaps repairable. What is it? What would it be useful for?" / "An old piece of machinery is discovered, cursed and dangerous. How does the community destroy it?" |
| 10 | "There's another community somewhere on the map. Where are they? What sets them apart from you?" / "What belief or practice helps to unify your community?" |
| J | "You see a good omen. What is it?" / "You see a bad omen. What is it?" |
| Q | "What's the most beautiful thing in this area?" / "What's the most hideous thing in this area?" |

([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

**Summer (♦)** — "Introduce a mystery at the edge of the map." / "An unattended situation becomes problematic and scary. What is it? How does it go awry?" (7); "Outsiders arrive in the area. Why are they a threat? How are they vulnerable?" / "Outsiders arrive in the area. How many? How are they greeted?" (6); "You discover a cache of supplies or resources. Add a new Abundance." / "A Scarcity has gone unaddressed for too long! Start a project that will alleviate that Scarcity." (10); "Summer is fleeting. Discard the top two cards of the top of the deck and take two actions this week." (K) ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

**Autumn (♣)** — "The Parish arrives. Who are they? Why have they chosen your community, and for what?" / "A small gang of marauders is making its way through local terrain. How many are there? What weapons do they carry?" (5); "Introduce a dark mystery among the members of the community." (6); "The community becomes obsessed with a single project. Which one? Why? Choose one: • They decide to take more time to ensure that it is perfect. Add 3 weeks to the project die. • They drop everything else to work on it. All other projects fail." (A); "A natural disaster strikes the area. What is it? Choose one: • You focus on getting everyone to safety. Remove an Abundance and a project fails. • You focus on protecting your supplies and hard work at any cost. Several people die as a result." (K) ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

**Winter (♠)** — "What is winter like in this area? How do community members react to the weather?" (7); "Winter is harsh, and desperation gives rise to fear mongering. Choose one: • Spend the week calming the masses and dispelling their violent sentiments. The week ends immediately. • Declare war on someone or something. This counts as starting a project." (8); "An infected outsider arrives, seeking amnesty. They have some much-needed resources with them. Choose one: • Welcome them into the community. Remove a Scarcity, but also introduce an infection into the community. • Bar them from entry. What Scarcity could they have addressed? How does its need become more dire this week?" (J); and the terminal card — "**The Frost Shepherds arrive. The game is over.**" (K) ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

The end condition is explicit: "The game ends abruptly when the King of Spades is drawn. The card is read aloud, and the game immediately concludes." ([Quiet Year PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf))

### What makes it gamified
- **Card draw** as the prompt-delivery mechanism, with suits creating a four-act seasonal arc.
- **Bold-text imperatives** on cards that override player choice (mandatory mechanical consequences).
- **Countdown dice** on the map as a visible progress clock per project.
- A **token economy** (20 Contempt Tokens) that converts social frustration into a spendable resource.
- A **randomised hard stop** — the King of Spades can come at any point in Winter, so the game's length is genuinely unknown.
- A **binary choice** structure on most cards ("or…"), converting a prompt into a decision.
- Hard **time limits** on outputs (30-second drawings, 1–2 sentence arguments, 2–3 minute turns).

### Direct relevance to Lit Technica
The strongest single model in this list for a Worldbuilding "prompt deck": a finite deck of two-option prompts, a seasonal/act progression, and countdown timers on in-progress world elements. The 30-second/1-sentence output caps are also a good anti-perfectionism device.

**URLs:** https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1518812420452.pdf (full-text mirror — non-primary)

## 3. Thousand Year Old Vampire (Tim Hutchings)

**Source caveat:** the full rules and Prompts 1–5 quoted below were extracted from a PDF mirror of v1.04 on pdfcoffee.com, a **non-primary aggregator**. ([TYOV v1.04 (mirror)](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

### What it is
A solo journaling RPG in which you record the millennium-long chronicle of a vampire by answering numbered Prompts in writing.

### How the mechanic works
Five traits: "Memories Skills Resources Characters Marks. Almost every time you receive a Prompt, one of your traits will be modified." ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

**Memories and Experiences — the core writing constraint:**

> "An Experience is a particular event; a Memory is an arc of Experiences that are tied together by subject or theme." ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

> "In game terms, an Experience is a single sentence that describes the resolution of a Prompt… An Experience should be a single evocative sentence. An Experience is the distillation of an event—a single sentence that combines what happened and why it matters to your vampire. A good format for an Experience is '[description of the event]; [how I feel or what I did about it].'" ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

> "Your vampire begins the game with space for five Memories, each of which can contain up to three Experiences." ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

> "If you have filled all five Memories and need to start a new one, strike out an existing Memory and all of the Experiences it contains—forgetting things is a fundamental aspect of the game so embrace it." ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

**The Diary escape valve:** "A Diary can hold up to four of your vampire's Memories… A Memory placed in the Diary is no longer in the vampire's head—it exists only in the Diary and the vampire accepts whatever is written as truth." Example Diary descriptions: "Diary – a sturdy, leather-bound book / Diary – a collection of pictogram-adorned pots / Diary – gold filigree signs bordering a frightening ritual mask / Diary – a password-protected forum on an archaic website" ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

**Skills:** "Swordplay, Relaxing Banter, Operate Heavy Machinery, I Do Not Blink The Sand Away, and I Teach the Nanissáanah are all acceptable Skills." And a beautiful design note: "If unchecked Skills are what your vampire is capable of, checked Skills are what they have done… revel in the plight of your humane vampire when they go to help a friend out of a bureaucratic jam and find that the only Skill they can check is Rage and Kill." ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

**Marks:** "A pair of great bat wings—I cut them off with a saw but the stubs remain; My eyes are hypnotic so I must wear smoked glasses, A dark halo I cover with tall military hats; Under the Moon my shadow takes the shape of a jaguar; Animals fear me, children cry" ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

**Character creation prompt sequence:** "Start by imagining a person in the distant past—a Roman emperor, a Mesopotamian midwife, a French knight… Start a Memory with one Experience that encapsulates their history. For instance: *I am Henri, son of Jon, born near the Loire Valley in the 13th Century of Our Lord; I am a poor knight swindled out of my inheritance.*" Then: three more Experiences each combining two traits (example given: "*Gundar takes me on my first voyage aboard the Longship Bøkesuden; his touch calms me when we first leave sight of land.*"), at least three Mortals, three Skills, three Resources, and finally one Immortal with a Mark — "*Baron Hollmueller, an Austrian noble and a vampire; he stole the deed to my land*" → "*I duel the eerie Baron Hollmueller across the roof of the abbey; he nearly cuts my head from my shoulders but I do not die*" → "*My neck is permanently broken, I wear tight scarves and walk slowly to maintain my dignity.*" ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

**The d10 − d6 progression rule (the signature mechanic):**

> "Roll your d10 and d6, and subtract the result of the d6 from that of the d10. If the result is a positive number, move forward that many Prompts; if it is a negative number, move backwards instead. A 0 means you encounter the same Prompt a second time… You will notice that Prompts have second and third entries. These are encountered the second and third times you land on a Prompt. If you land on a Prompt and have already responded to all the entries, move along to the next Prompt." ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

**End condition:** "The Game Ends… if you are unable to check or lose a Skill or Resource when required to do so, or if a Prompt tells you that the game has ended." ([TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

### Verbatim Prompts (1–5, all layers)

**Prompt 1**
1. "In your blood-hunger you destroy someone close to you. Kill a mortal Character. Create a mortal if none are available. Take the skill Bloodthirsty."
2. "Horrified at your new nature, you withdraw from society. Where do you hide? How do you feed? Create a stationary Resource which shelters you."
3. "You are overcome by panic and maul someone close to you, accidentally turning them into a monster like yourself. Convert a beloved mortal Character into an enemy immortal. Take the Skill Ashamed."

**Prompt 2**
1. "You reinvent your existence around the seclusion of your hiding place. You begin to work in an artful way, changing your living environment. How do you come to appreciate beauty or craft in a new way? Create a Skill based on a Memory."
2. "You are captured and enslaved by a wicked and powerful supernatural entity. Create an immortal Character. How do you eventually escape their servitude? Check a Skill and take the Skill Humans are Cattle. Strike out all mortal Characters, as a hundred years have passed. Take a Resource you have used for evil while in service to your former master."
3. "Your hiding place is destroyed by mortals. What steps had you taken to ensure your survival? What revenge do you wreak upon your persecutors? Degrade a Resource into ruins. Take the Skill Vile Acts."

**Prompt 3**
1. "A loved one discovers your condition and works to help you. Create a Resource which represents their assistance. Create a mortal Character if none are available."
2. "You are exposed and flee to a neighboring region. Lose any stationary Resources. Check a Skill. A mortal flees with you. What new name do you adopt among these strangers?"
3. "You manipulate this mortal into committing atrocious deeds on your behalf. What do you do when they quail at these awful tasks? Take the Skill Humans are Tools."

**Prompt 4**
1. "You are adopted into a strange cult who take you in despite (or because of) your outlander origin. Check a Skill and create a Resource, The Secret Cabal. How did they find you? What vile initiation ceremony do you undergo? Do they know what you are?"
2. "At the end of the mortal's life you convert them into a mindless meat machine, an undying thing from which you feed. What regrets do you have? Change the Character to a Resource. Check a Skill."
3. "The Secret Cabal, without your knowledge, performs a dark ceremony that changes a mortal Character into a horrific, alien and immortal thing. Convert a mortal Character into an immortal enemy. What alien objectives does this new immortal pursue? Did the Cabal manipulate you into helping with this creation? How does this change your relationship to the Cabal?"

**Prompt 5**
1. "You murder someone you love or respect rather than let them expose you. Kill a Character. Check a Skill. If you have no living Characters, kill no one and create a beloved mortal Character who you have betrayed."
2. "A mortal Character begins serving you. Who are they? Why are they drawn to you? Create a new mortal Character."
3. "A Character you've victimized comes to you in a dream. Do they curse or forgive you? Receive a Mark."

(All from [TYOV](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html))

A published actual-play account of the game is available for tone reference ([Martin Ralya — RPG actual play: Thousand Year Old Vampire](https://www.martinralya.com/tabletop-rpgs/rpg-actual-play-thousand-year-old-vampire/)).

### What makes it gamified
- **Non-linear prompt traversal** via d10 − d6: you can move backwards, revisit, and skip.
- **Layered prompts** — each prompt has up to three entries, consumed in order on repeat visits.
- **Finite, destructive inventory**: five Memory slots forces deletion of earlier fiction; struck-out entries stay legible.
- **Trait bookkeeping** (check / strike out) with a substitution rule (Skill ↔ Resource) that acts as a hidden hit-point pool.
- A **loss condition** derived from bookkeeping exhaustion rather than narrative choice.
- A strict **one-sentence output format** with a stated template.

### Direct relevance to Lit Technica
Two portable ideas. First, the **Experience template** — "[description of the event]; [how I feel or what I did about it]" — is a compact, teachable craft rule for scene beats or character backstory entries. Second, the **finite memory slots** mechanic: a Character workspace could offer an optional "five memories only" constraint that forces the writer to decide what their character has forgotten, which is a genuine characterisation tool rather than a gimmick.

## 4. Ironsworn and Ironsworn: Starforged (Shawn Tomkin)

Ironsworn is explicitly designed for solo, co-op and guided play. Its oracle system is the most directly reusable "ask a question, get a nudge" engine in this list.

### 4a. Ironsworn — Ask the Oracle

**Source:** an SRD copy hosted on GitHub Pages (the canonical `ironswornsrd.com` failed to resolve during this session — see failures list). ([Ironsworn SRD (mirror)](https://tedtschopp.github.io/Ironsworn-SRD/Ironsworn%20SRD.html))

The move, verbatim:

> "When you seek to resolve questions, discover details in the world, determine how other characters respond, or trigger encounters or events, you may…
> - Draw a conclusion: Decide the answer based on the most interesting and obvious result.
> - Ask a yes/no question: Decide the odds of a 'yes', and roll on the table below to check the answer.
> - Pick two: Envision two options. Rate one as 'likely', and roll on the table below to see if it is true. If not, it is the other.
> - Spark an idea: Brainstorm or use a random prompt." ([Ironsworn SRD](https://tedtschopp.github.io/Ironsworn-SRD/Ironsworn%20SRD.html))

| Odds | The answer is 'yes' if you roll… |
|---|---|
| Almost Certain | 11 or greater |
| Likely | 26 or greater |
| 50/50 | 51 or greater |
| Unlikely | 76 or greater |
| Small Chance | 91 or greater |

> "On a match, an extreme result or twist has occurred." ([Ironsworn SRD](https://tedtschopp.github.io/Ironsworn-SRD/Ironsworn%20SRD.html))

**Swear an Iron Vow / Fulfill Your Vow** turn a goal into a tracked object:

> "When you swear upon iron to complete a quest, write your vow and give the quest a rank." On a strong hit "you are emboldened and it is clear what you must do next (Ask the Oracle if unsure)"; on a weak hit "you are determined but begin your quest with more questions than answers"; on a miss "you face a significant obstacle before you can begin your quest." ([Ironsworn SRD](https://tedtschopp.github.io/Ironsworn-SRD/Ironsworn%20SRD.html))

On failing to complete a vow: "You recommit: Clear all but one filled progress, and raise the quest's rank by one (if not already epic)." ([Ironsworn SRD](https://tedtschopp.github.io/Ironsworn-SRD/Ironsworn%20SRD.html))

**Progress tracks** — the scaling mechanic:

### What makes it gamified
- **Calibrated probability**: the writer states how likely they *think* the answer is, then the dice can overrule them — this preserves authorial intent while allowing surprise.
- The **match/twist rule** (doubles = extreme result) injects escalation without extra bookkeeping.
- **Word-pair oracles** (verb+noun, adjective+noun) deliberately produce ambiguous prompts requiring interpretation rather than dictating content.
- **Progress tracks + rank scaling** make big goals mechanically slower, and **recommitting** to a failed vow *raises* its rank — failure escalates stakes rather than ending them.
- **XP tied to difficulty rank** rewards ambition.

### Direct relevance to Lit Technica
The "Ask the Oracle" odds ladder is the single most transferable widget here: a writer stuck on a plot question picks Almost Certain / Likely / 50-50 / Unlikely / Small Chance, and the app returns Yes/No plus an occasional "and there's a twist". The Descriptor+Focus and Action+Theme pairs are ideal for a low-friction "Spark an idea" button in the Worldbuilding workspace. Settlement Trouble is effectively a ready-made "what's wrong with this place?" generator.

## 5. Dungeon World / Apocalypse World lineage — Fronts, GM Agenda & Principles, Bonds

**Source:** the Dungeon World SRD, published under Creative Commons by the game's authors. ([Dungeon World SRD — Gamemastering](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/) ; [Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))

### 5a. GM Agenda and Principles — prompts disguised as instructions

**Agenda (three items only):**
> "Portray a fantastic world / Fill the characters' lives with adventure / Play to find out what happens" — "Everything you say and do at the table (and away from the table, too) exists to accomplish these three goals and no others… You're most certainly not here to tell everyone a planned-out story." ([DW SRD](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/))

**Principles (12):**
> "Draw maps, leave blanks / Address the characters, not the players / Embrace the fantastic / Make a move that follows / Never speak the name of your move / Give every monster life / Name every person / Ask questions and use the answers / Be a fan of the characters / Think dangerous / Begin and end with the fiction / Think offscreen, too" ([DW SRD](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/))

**GM Moves (12)** — a menu consulted at moments of uncertainty:
> "Use a monster, danger, or location move / Reveal an unwelcome truth / Show signs of an approaching threat / Deal damage / Use up their resources / Turn their move back on them / Separate them / Give an opportunity that fits a class' abilities / Show a downside to their class, race, or equipment / Offer an opportunity, with or without cost / Put someone in a spot / Tell them the requirements or consequences and ask" ([DW SRD](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/))

Trigger conditions and the soft/hard distinction:
> "You make a move: When everyone looks to you to find out what happens / When the players give you a golden opportunity / When they roll a 6-." And: "A soft move is one without immediate, irrevocable consequences… A soft move ignored becomes a golden opportunity for a hard move." ([DW SRD](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/))

Also: "No matter what move you make, always follow up with 'What do you do?'" ([DW SRD](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/))

### 5b. Fronts — a structured antagonist/plot template

> "Fronts are secret tomes of GM knowledge. Each is a collection of linked dangers—threats to the characters specifically and to the people, places, and things the characters care about. It also includes one or more impending dooms, the horrible things that will happen without the characters' intervention. 'Fronts' comes, of course, from 'fighting on two fronts'…" ([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))

**The build checklist, verbatim:**
> "- Choose campaign front or adventure front
> - Create 2-3 dangers
> - Choose an impending doom for each danger
> - Add grim portents (1-3 for an adventure front, 3-5 for the campaign front)
> - Write 1–3 stakes questions
> - List the general cast of the front" ([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))

**Danger types and impulses** (an excellent ready-made antagonist table). Selected verbatim rows:

| Type | Impulse |
|---|---|
| Misguided Good | to do what is "right" no matter the cost |
| Thieves Guild | to take by subterfuge |
| Cult | to infest from within |
| Corrupt Government | to maintain the status quo |
| Cabal | to absorb those in power, to grow |
| God | to gather worshipers |
| Demon Prince | to open the gates of Hell |
| Elemental Lord | to tear down creation to its component parts |
| Force of Chaos | to destroy all semblance of order |
| Choir of Angels | to pass judgment |
| Lord of the Undead | to seek true immortality |
| Sentient Artifact | to find a worthy wielder |
| Ancient Curse | to ensnare |
| Chosen One | to fulfill or resent their destiny |
| Dragon | to hoard gold and jewels, to protect the clutch |
| Humanoid Vermin | to breed, to multiply and consume |
| Plague of the Undead | to spread |
| Abandoned Tower | to draw in the weak-willed |
| Unholy Ground | to spawn evil |
| Dark Portal | to disgorge demons |
| Shadowland | to corrupt or consume the living |
| Place of Power | to be controlled or tamed |

([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))

**Grim portents** — the timeline mechanic:
> "Grim portents are dark designs for what could happen if a danger goes unchecked… A grim portent can be a single interesting event or a chain of steps. When you're not sure what to do next, push your danger towards resolving a grim portent." ([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))

> "When a grim portent comes to pass, check it off—the prophecy has come true!" And crucially: "You can advance a grim portent descriptively or prescriptively. Descriptively means that you've seen the change happen during play, so you mark it off… Prescriptive is when, due to a failed player move or a golden opportunity, you advance the grim portent as your hard move." ([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))

**Impending dooms (choose one per danger):** "Tyranny (of the strong over the weak or the few over the many) / Pestilence (the spread of sickness and disease, the end of wellness) / Destruction (apocalypse, ruin and woe) / Usurpation (the chain of order comes apart, someone rightful is displaced) / Impoverishment (enslavement, the abandonment of goodness and right)" ([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))

**Stakes questions** — the most writer-relevant part:
> "Your stakes questions are 1-3 questions about people, places, or groups that you're interested in… Stakes are a way of reminding yourself what you want to find out." ([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))

> "Stakes are concrete and clear. Don't write stakes about vague feelings or incremental changes. Stakes are about important changes that affect the PCs and the world. A good stakes question is one that, when it's resolved, means that things will never be the same again." ([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))

> "Your stakes should be things that you genuinely want to know, but that you're also willing to leave to be resolved through play. Once you've written it as a stake, it's out of your hands, you don't get to just make it up anymore." ([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))

**Worked example — "The Opening of the White Gate"** ([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/)):

- *The College of Arcanists* (Cabal), impulse "to absorb those in power, to grow". Grim portents: "The College sends an expedition to the Gate / The Key is discovered / The Gate's Power is harnessed / The College seizes control". Impending Doom: **Usurpation**.
- *The White Gate* (Dark Portal), impulse "to disgorge demons". Grim portents: "The First Trumpet sounds / The Second Trumpet sounds / The Gate is opened". Impending Doom: **Destruction**.
- *The Argent Seraphim* (Choir of Angels), impulse "to pass judgment". Grim portents: "A Champion is chosen / An organization of power is formed or co-opted / The Herald appears / Judgment is passed". Impending Doom: **Tyranny**.
- Description: "An ancient gate, buried for aeons in the icy north. It opens into a realm of pure light, guarded by the Argent Seraphim. It was crafted only to be opened at Judgment Day, so that the Seraphim could come forth and purge the realm of men. It was recently uncovered by the College of Arcanists, who do not yet understand its terrible power."
- Cast: "Oren Balserus, Arcanist Supreme / Hali'el, voice of the Seraphim / Drudge, a manservant"
- Stakes: "Who will be the Champion? / How will Lux respond to the holy light? / Will the College be able to recruit Avon?"

### 5c. Bonds — relational character prompts as fill-in-the-blank

Each playbook ships with a short list of relationship statements. Instruction: "Fill in the name of one of your companions in at least one:"

- **Thief:** "I stole something from [name]." / "[name] has my back when things go wrong." / "[name] knows incriminating details about me." / "I have conned [name]." ([DW SRD — Thief](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/thief/))
- **Wizard:** "___ will play an important role in the events to come. I have foreseen it!" / "___ is keeping an important secret from me." / "___ is woefully misinformed about the world; I will teach them all that I can." ([DW SRD — Wizard](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/wizard/))
- **Ranger:** "I have guided ___ before and they owe me for it." / "___ is a good and faithful person. I trust them implicitly." / "___ is a danger to themselves and others. I will keep them safe." / "I have a special connection to ___." ([DW SRD — Ranger](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/ranger/))
- **Paladin:** "I have sworn to protect ______." / "I have shown ______ the error of their ways." / "______ is a brave soul, I have much to learn from them." / "I respect the beliefs of ______ but hope they will someday see the true way." / "______ has stood by me in battle and can be trusted completely." ([DW SRD — Paladin](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/paladin/))

Playbooks also carry **Look** menus — pick-one-from-each visual prompts. Thief: "Hooded, scarred, or disheveled face / Sharp eyes, dead eyes, or calculating eyes / Worn clothing, dark clothing, or fancy clothing / Lithe body, hunched body, or bony body" ([DW SRD — Thief](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/thief/)). Paladin: "A man, a woman, or an ambiguous gender / Battered helm, shining helm, or no helm / Ceremonial armor, practical armor, or ancient armor / A holy symbol, a shield, or a sword / A noble bearing, a common bearing, or a mysterious bearing" ([DW SRD — Paladin](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/paladin/)). Ranger: "Wild eyes, sharp eyes, or animal eyes / Wild hair, cropped hair, or hooded head / Worn leathers, camouflage, or battered furs / Lithe body, rugged body, or sharp body" ([DW SRD — Ranger](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/ranger/)). Wizard: "Haunted Eyes, Sharp Eyes, or Crazy Eyes / Styled Hair, Wild Hair, or Pointed Hat / Worn Robes, Stylish Robes, or Strange Robes / Pudgy Body, Creepy Body, or Thin Body" ([DW SRD — Wizard](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/wizard/)). Name lists are also provided by ancestry, e.g. Thief halfling names "Felix, Strea, Hywn, Trixie, Robin, Pox, Omen, Shy, Zeke, Rask, Trix, Daph" ([DW SRD — Thief](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/thief/)).

### What makes it gamified
- A **checklist-shaped template** for antagonists (2–3 dangers → doom → portents → stakes → cast) with hard numeric limits.
- **Checkbox progression**: grim portents are literally ticked off, giving a plot a visible completion bar.
- **Impulse** as a one-line behavioural rule that mechanically answers "what does this faction do next?"
- A finite **move menu** consulted when the author is stuck, with a soft/hard escalation gradient.
- **Bonds** as fill-in-the-blank templates producing pre-loaded inter-character tension.
- Constraint by omission: "Draw maps, leave blanks" and "Ask questions and use the answers" institutionalise deliberate under-specification.

### Direct relevance to Lit Technica
Fronts are essentially an antagonist-and-timeline template, mapping cleanly onto the Plot and Timeline workspaces: a threat, a doom it's driving toward, a checkable list of escalating portents, and 1–3 open questions the author has promised not to pre-decide. The **stakes question rule** ("once you've written it as a stake, it's out of your hands") is exactly the kind of optional discipline a craft library can offer. Bonds are the model for a Character workspace relationship-prompt deck.

## 6. Traveller — the classic Lifepath career system

**Source:** the Traveller SRD (Mongoose Traveller, published under the Traveller Open Game Licence). ([Traveller SRD — Careers](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/careers/))

### What it is
Character generation as a playable mini-game: you live your character through four-year *terms*, rolling for qualification, skills, survival, events and promotion, until you leave and muster out. Ageing and injury can happen before play even begins.

### How the mechanic works
The dice convention: "Most of these throws are characteristic throws – roll 2d6, add the DM from the listed characteristic, and try to get a total higher than the listed value. A throw of Int 8+ means 'roll 2d6, add your Intelligence DM, and you succeed if you roll an 8 or more'." ([Traveller SRD](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/careers/))

The per-term loop, verbatim from the Career Format section ([Traveller SRD](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/careers/)):

- **Qualification** — "What you need to roll to enter that career… If you fail this check then you cannot enter your chosen career this term. You must either submit to the Draft or take the Drifter career for this term. You suffer a -1 DM to qualification rolls for each previous career you have entered. Once you leave a career you cannot return to it."
- **Basic Training** — "For your first career only, you get all the skills listed in the Service Skills table at Level 0 as your basic training."
- **Skills and Training** — "In each term you spend in a career, pick one of these tables and roll 1d6 to see which skill you increase."
- **Survival** — "Each career has a survival roll. If you fail this roll, roll on the mishap table. This mishap is always enough to force you to leave the service… A natural 2 is always a failure."
- **Events** — "If you are still in your career after resolving the survival roll, roll on the events table to see what interesting things befall you this term."
- **Advancement** — "If your result is equal to or less than the number of terms you have spent in this career, then you cannot continue in this career after this term… If you roll a natural 12, then you must continue in this career."
- **Mustering-Out Benefits** — "When you leave a career for any reason, you gain material benefits from that career."

### Verbatim table entries — the Scout career

**Enlistment:** "Int 5+ -1 DM for every previous career." Assignments: Courier / Survey / Exploration ([Traveller SRD](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/careers/)).

**Mishaps (1d6)** — narrative failure states, each a story seed:
| 1d6 | Mishap |
|---|---|
| 1 | "Severely injured in action. (This is the same as a result of 2 on the Injury table.) Alternatively, roll twice on the Injury table and take the lower result." |
| 2 | "Psychologically damaged by your time in the scouts. Reduce your Intelligence or Social Standing by 1." |
| 3 | "Your ship is damaged, and you have to hitch-hike your way back across the stars to the nearest scout base. Gain 1d6 Contacts and 1d3 Enemies." |
| 4 | "You inadvertently cause a conflict between the Imperium and a minor world or race. Gain a Rival and Diplomat 1." |
| 5 | "You have no idea what happened to you – they found your ship drifting on the fringes of friendly space." |
| 6 | "Injured. Roll on the Injury table." |

**Events (2d6)** — selected verbatim:
| 2d6 | Event |
|---|---|
| 2 | "Disaster! Roll on the mishap table, but you are not ejected from this career." |
| 3 | "Your ship is ambushed by enemy vessels. Either run, and throw Pilot 8+ to escape, or treat with them and throw Persuade 10+ to bargain with them. If you fail the check, then your ship is destroyed and you may not re-enlist in the Scouts at the end of this term. If you succeed, you survive and gain Sensors 1. Either way, gain an Enemy." |
| 4 | "You survey an alien world. Gain one of Animals (riding or training) 1, Survival 1, Recon 1 or Life Science (any) 1" |
| 7 | "Life Event. Roll on the Life Events table." |
| 9 | "Your scout ship is one of the first on the scene to rescue the survivors of a disaster. Roll either Medic 8+ or Engineer 8+. If you succeed, gain a Contact and a +2 DM to your next Advancement check. If you fail, gain an Enemy." |
| 11 | "You serve as the courier for an important message from the Imperium. Either gain one level of Diplomat, or take a +4 DM to your next Advancement roll." |
| 12 | "You discover a world, item or information of worth to the Imperium. You are automatically promoted." |

([Traveller SRD](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/careers/))

**Ageing** — the mechanic that most famously gives Traveller characters a *history*:
> "The effects of ageing begin when a character reaches 34 years of age. At the end of the fourth term, and at the end of every term thereafter, the character must roll 2d6 on the Ageing Table. Apply the character's total number of terms as a negative Dice Modifier on this table." Results run from "1+ No effect" down to "-6 Reduce three physical characteristics by 2, reduce one mental characteristic by 1". "**Ageing Crisis**: If any characteristic is reduced to 0 by ageing, then the character suffers an ageing crisis. The character dies unless he can pay 1d6 x 10,000 credits for medical care…" ([Traveller SRD](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/careers/))

**Injury table (1d6):** "1 Nearly killed… / 2 Severely injured… / 3 Missing eye or limb. Reduce Strength or Dexterity by 2. / 4 Scarred. You are scarred and injured… / 5 Injured… / 6 Lightly injured. No permanent effect." ([Traveller SRD](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/careers/))

**Connections** (a group-level relational prompt): "At this stage, you may make (or finalise) a connection between your character and up to two other player characters. For each connection you make, you may gain one level in any skill…" ([Traveller SRD](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/careers/))

Failing to qualify sends you to the Draft (1d6: "Navy (any) / Army (any) / Marines (any) / Merchants (merchant marine) / Scouts (any) / Agent (law enforcement)") or the Drifter career ([Traveller SRD](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/careers/)).

### What makes it gamified
- **Push-your-luck loop**: each additional term earns skills and benefits but risks a mishap, ageing and death.
- Failure is **generative, not terminal** — a mishap gives you "1d6 Contacts and 1d3 Enemies" or an unexplained gap in memory, i.e. plot hooks.
- **Escalating penalties** on repeated attempts (-1 DM per previous career), and an advancement rule that can force you out ("equal to or less than the number of terms you have spent in this career").
- **Emergent biography**: the sequence of rolls *is* the backstory. Characters arrive with rivals, debts, scars and pensions.
- **Named forced outcomes** — the natural 12 that means "you must continue in this career" produces an involuntary lifer.

### Direct relevance to Lit Technica
The Character workspace analogue is a "life path" generator: the writer steps a character through decades, and each step offers an optional event roll producing a Contact, an Enemy, a scar, or an unexplained gap. The critical design lesson is that **failure results are the most interesting outputs** — a backstory generator should be biased toward complications, not achievements.

## 7. The Story Engine Deck and Deck of Worlds (Peter Chiykowski / Rocketship Games)

### 7a. The Story Engine Deck

> "The Story Engine™ is a deck of 180 cards for creating storytelling prompts for fiction, art, role-playing games, and more." ([The Story Engine Deck Guidebook (PDF)](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

> "The Story Engine™ has two rules:
> 1) Don't put the deck away until you create something. It can just be a word in a notebook or an idea in your head.
> 2) Ignore any card, rule, or guideline that doesn't help you create.
>
> The deck is meant to serve your creative goals—not the other way around. Ditch the rules when they get in the way." ([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

**Five card types:**
| Card type | Description | Number of cues |
|---|---|---|
| 1 AGENTS | "are characters who make choices in the story." | Four |
| 2 ENGINES | "are motivations and relationships that drive the story." | Two |
| 3 ANCHORS | "are objects, locations, or events of importance." | Four |
| 4 CONFLICTS | "are obstacles, consequences, or dilemmas." | Two |
| 5 ASPECTS | "are adjectives that describe other cards." | Four |

([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

> "The sets of white words on the edge of each card are called cues. When you lay out cards, the cue facing you is the one that is active in the prompt." ([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

**Three physical verbs:** "**Place:** Put a card on the table faceup… **Tuck:** Place a card faceup under another card so that one cue from the bottom card is visible. **Rotate:** Turn a placed or tucked card so a new cue is facing you." ([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

**"Story Seed" — the simplest prompt:** "A) Draw and place 1 (1 Agent) to create a character. B) Draw and place 2 (1 Engine) to give them a motivation. C) Draw and place 3 (1 Anchor) to create something they want. D) Draw and place 4 (1 Conflict) to create an obstacle or consequence. E) Draw and tuck 5 (1 Aspect) to add detail to a card." ([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

**Verbatim example prompts produced by the system:**
- "A survivor wants to expose the secret of a corrupted paradise but their community will reject them." — with the interpretive gloss: "This prompt might inspire a story about an apocalypse survivor who finds a seemingly perfect haven community with a dark secret and must decide whether to dig deeper. Or an ageing war veteran who moves into an idyllic senior's residence, only to discover it's being used to launder money."
- "A decrepit prophet with a fiery shield wants to steal from an impulsive rival but it will mean breaking a promise."
- "A long-lost book in a cave owned by a private witch will end the power of a besieged city but it will cost their (the witch's) self-respect."
- "A demon wants to defeat an archaeologist but it will cost them (the demon) their mental health. The archaeologist wants to control the demon but the innocent will suffer." (Circle of Fate)
- "A bloody god wants to destroy the world with a dire contagion but they must face their deepest fear. A wounded villain wants to find redemption in the dire contagion but they may lose their life." (A Soul Divided)
- "An honest criminal wants to upstage a rival with a passionate letter but also wants to find a way back to their twin."
- Competitive play example — Placer's prompt: "An architect wants to escape an unlucky village but it will mean betraying family." / Rotator's prompt: "An artisan wants to find an unlucky boat but it will mean forgiving family."

([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

**"Changing a Prompt" — an unblocking toolkit** (each is a named micro-move with a reflective question):
- "**Rotate:** Rotate a card to a new cue and try re-interpreting the cards. Does it help to think of the cues more, or less, literally?"
- "**Relocate:** Move an 5 Aspect to a different card and optionally rotate it. Do you see that card in a new light?"
- "**Salvage:** Take a card from your discards and use it as a substitute for a card in the prompt. Does it help?"
- "**Furnish:** Draw and tuck 3 under an existing 3 Anchor or 1 Agent. Does adding a location, prop, or personal possession to the existing card help bring the prompt into focus?"
- "**Bestow:** Draw and tuck 1 under an existing 3 Anchor to suggest an owner for it. Do story opportunities emerge if a character is connected to the object?"
- "**Specify:** If a 4 Conflict or 2 Engine leaves something open-ended (like referring to a 'secret' or an 'enemy'), draw and tuck 1 or 3 or 5 to fill in that detail. Is the prompt clearer?"
- "**Diversify:** Draw and tuck 1 under an existing 1 Agent to add a new dimension to their character. How does this change their options or role in the story?"
- "**Simplify:** Remove a tucked card. Does this give you more room to interpret?"
- "**Combine:** Turn an 2 Engine or 4 Conflict sideways to indicate that both cues apply. Your character now feels two conflicting motivations toward the same thing, or must choose between two obstacles/consequences."
- "**Reverse:** Reverse the placement of two cards on either side of an 2 Engine… How does the dynamic change? Can you tell the story this way?"

([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

**Locking cards — integrating a prompt with pre-existing fiction.** This is the mechanic most directly relevant to an app where the user already has a manuscript:
> "You may want to create a prompt that includes a story element you have already created, such as the main character from your novel or a location from your RPG campaign. This is called locking a card… When you reach that point in creating the prompt, place that card facedown. The facedown card represents your pre-existing story element." ([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

Worked example: "The player in the previous example might lock the 3 Anchor in Step C to represent their main character's hometown, a seaside village called Gullport. Now, a new character (a ghostly survivor) wants to expose the secret of Gullport but their community will reject them." ([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

**Multiplayer modes** — Comparative Storytelling splits authority between a **placer** and a **rotator** ("The player who most recently read a book starts in the role of placer"), then: "When the prompt is complete, the players spend 5 minutes (or a set amount of time) writing a story or story notes… Players compare and discuss how they used the prompt differently." Collaborative Storytelling gives each turn one of three verbs — Interpret, Change, Expand — with the example interpretation: "This A GUARDIAN 1 Agent with a FIERY 5 Aspect is Captain Conniption, a superhero with an anger problem." ([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

An explicit permission clause: "You do not need to use the actual words from your cues in the story or idea you create." ([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))

### 7b. Deck of Worlds

> "Deck of Worlds contains six types of cards you can combine to create unique fictional settings. Each card features one or more cues, which are optional worldbuilding elements or ideas you can include in your setting." ([Deck of Worlds Guidebook (PDF)](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf))

> "When you combine all six types of cards together, the resulting cluster is called a microsetting. Microsettings are distinct areas in your world with their own past, present, and future lore." ([Deck of Worlds Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf))

**Six card types, verbatim:**
| # | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Region | "Regions establish a microsetting's main terrain type and act as a hub for arranging other cards." |
| 2 | Landmark | "Landmarks add geographical sites and points of interest." |
| 3 | Namesake | "Namesakes combine with Regions or Landmarks to create in-world nicknames." |
| 4 | Origin | "Origins record significant events of the area's past." |
| 5 | Attribute | "Attributes highlight present-day features of the area and its people." |
| 6 | Advent | "Advents introduce events that may change the area's future. Each cue provides optional interpretation suggestions in smaller text." |

([Deck of Worlds Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf))

Note the temporal design: Origin = past, Attribute = present, Advent = future. That is a three-slot timeline baked into a setting generator.

**Simple microsetting build sequence, verbatim:**
- Step A: "Draw 1 (1 Region card). Place it anywhere on the table. This provides the underlying environment type of your microsetting."
- Step B: "Draw 2 (1 Landmark card) and tuck it under any edge of the 1 Region so only one cue is showing. This expands the area of your microsetting and provides a point of interest."
- Step C: "Draw 3 (1 Namesake card) and tuck it under the 1 Region or 2 Landmark so only one cue is showing. This provides an in-world nickname."
- Step D: "Draw 4 (1 Origin card) and tuck it under any edge… The 4 Origin provides a lore-based backstory."
- Step E: "Draw 5 (1 Attribute card) and tuck it under any edge… The 5 Attribute provides a present-day detail about the area or its people."
- Step F: "Draw 6 (1 Advent card) and tuck it under any edge… The 6 Advent provides an event that could change the future of the area and/or serve as a story hook."

([Deck of Worlds Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf))

**Worked example wording:** "There is a 'SETTLEMENT' in this 'SWAMP' microsetting." → "The 'SWAMP' is nicknamed **'The Swamp Where No Bird Sings.'**" → "The 'SETTLEMENT' was the site of a famous scientific discovery." → "The 'SWAMP' is known for its fossils." → "A land dispute is underway in the microsetting." → "The microsetting is complete!" ([Deck of Worlds Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf))

Interpretation notes from the same example: "Called 'The Swamp Where No Bird Sings' due to an extinct bird species that used to be abundant." / "Contains a settlement founded on the site where the bird fossils were first discovered. Biologists now occupy it, exhuming fossils and specimens preserved by the swamp." / "The land belongs to a nearby kingdom, which just sold it to a foreign power with plans to develop it. The biologists are protesting the sale." ([Deck of Worlds Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf))

A second worked example — "Called 'The Meandering Desert' due to its winding trails and long, dry growing season." with "A farmer started a vineyard using a stolen inheritance (despite the arid climate). Has worked the land for generations, but their breed of vine is going extinct. Disease? Drought? Curse? Characters can investigate." and "Also home to a commune of stained glass artists called 'The Workshop That Will Never Die.' Their kilns run all day, every day, because they believe in 'keeping the flame of eternity' What do they use for fuel? Is pollution affecting the vineyard?" ([Deck of Worlds Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf))

**Namesake grammar** — a genuinely clever naming generator: "The top cue of the 3 Namesake is designed to be tucked at the top edge of a 1 Region and read as though it begins the nickname. Each of the three lower cues can be tucked under a 1 Region or 2 Landmark and read as though it ends the name." Cross-deck examples: 'FOREST' + 'A MERCHANT' = "The Merchant's Forest"; 'CITY' + 'A TAILOR' = "The City of Tailors"; 'VALLEY' + 'A RUIN' = "The Valley of Ruins"; 'RIVER' + 'GENTLE' = "The Gentle River" ([Deck of Worlds Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf)).

**Map-scale guidance** (useful for a Worldbuilding workspace with a map view):
| Map scale | Microsettings | Space | Distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large continent-sized map | 14-20 | 30" x 25" | "Treat 1" as approximately 50 miles (80 km)." |
| Medium country-sized map | 9-14 | 25" x 20" | "Treat 1" as approximately 20 miles (32 km)." |
| Small city-sized detail map | 5-7 | 20" x 15" | "Treat 1" as approximately 1 mile (1.6 km)." |

Plus: "Travel speed: Some tabletop RPGs estimate that the average person can travel 24 miles in a day. Some historical sources estimate the speed of foot travel in the Middle Ages at 9-14 miles per day." ([Deck of Worlds Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf))

Zoom-out permission: "you might draw the 'NO WRITTEN LANGUAGE' 5 Attribute, and decide that your entire world has developed without the need to write anything down. Or you might draw the 'OF GLASS' 3 Namesake and decide to name your world 'The Continent of Glass.'" ([Deck of Worlds Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf))

### 7c. Rory's Story Cubes (Asmodee / Zygomatic)

Nine dice, rolled and interpreted. Official rules, verbatim:

> "**How to play.** When playing with family and friends take turns as the storyteller. The storyteller rolls all 9 cubes. Begin with 'Once Upon a Time' and tell a story that links together all 9 face-up images. Start with the first image to grab your attention. Use three cubes for the beginning, three for the middle and three for the end of the story. There is one rule: there are no wrong answers." ([Rory's Story Cubes Classic official rules PDF](https://www.zygomatic-games.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/storycubesclassic_en_rules.pdf))

> "**My Hero.** Roll three cubes and use them to create a character. Now roll all nine cubes and tell a story featuring your hero. What will happen when your hero finds herself at the pyramids in Ancient Egypt, or with a turtle for a sidekick?" ([Zygomatic rules PDF](https://www.zygomatic-games.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/storycubesclassic_en_rules.pdf))

> "**Epic Stories.** The first player rolls all nine cubes and weaves the opening chapter. Taking turns, each consecutive player adds a chapter to the story. The last player must tie up all the loose ends and bring the story to its epic conclusion. Add different sets of Rory's Story Cubes® to give your stories a whole new dimension." ([Zygomatic rules PDF](https://www.zygomatic-games.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/storycubesclassic_en_rules.pdf))

> "**Stuck for ideas?** Pick your favourite movie, book or TV show. What new and imaginative stories can you spin using these characters and settings?" ([Zygomatic rules PDF](https://www.zygomatic-games.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/storycubesclassic_en_rules.pdf))

The publisher's site adds interpretive permissions: "You're free to interpret the symbols — They can represent different things for everyone, and the first idea you have is often the right one." and "If you're nervous, just go for it. There are no bad interpretations, and there's no such thing as a bad story." ([Story Cubes — How to play](https://www.storycubes.com/en/comment-jouer/)). It also notes the three-act split as a *tip* rather than a rule: "To create your story, you can use three dice for the setup, three for the story development, and three for the conclusion." ([Story Cubes](https://www.storycubes.com/en/comment-jouer/)). (Nine cubes × six faces = 54 images; the frequently-quoted "over 10 million combinations" figure could **not** be confirmed from a fetched publisher page in this session — treat as **n.a.**)

### What makes these gamified
- **Card draw + physical arrangement** (place / tuck / rotate) — the same cards yield different prompts depending on orientation, so re-rolling is cheap.
- **Draw-more-than-you-need + discards**, preserving player agency inside randomness.
- **Named remix moves** (Rotate, Relocate, Salvage, Furnish, Bestow, Specify, Diversify, Simplify, Combine, Reverse) — a structured unblocking vocabulary.
- **Locking** cards to bind randomness to existing fiction.
- **Role-splitting** in multiplayer (placer vs rotator) and a **timed writing sprint** (5 minutes) followed by comparison.
- Story Cubes' **fixed three-act allocation** (3 setup / 3 middle / 3 end) turns nine random icons into a structured arc.
- Explicit **anti-failure framing** in both products ("there are no wrong answers"; "Ignore any card, rule, or guideline that doesn't help you create").

### Direct relevance to Lit Technica
This is the closest commercial analogue to the intended feature and the best model for tone. Three things to steal (conceptually, with attribution): (1) the **"ignore any card that doesn't help you"** rule as the explicit framing for a non-mandatory prompt deck; (2) **locking** — letting the writer pin an existing character/location from their manuscript into a generated prompt; (3) the **remix verbs** — instead of one "reroll" button, offer Rotate / Specify / Simplify / Combine as distinct nudges. Deck of Worlds' past/present/future triad (Origin / Attribute / Advent) is a ready-made schema for Worldbuilding entries.

## 8. Oblique Strategies (Brian Eno & Peter Schmidt, 1975)

### What it is
> "**Oblique Strategies** … subtitled 'Over One Hundred Worthwhile Dilemmas' … is 'a card-based method for promoting creativity jointly created by musician/artist Brian Eno and multimedia artist Peter Schmidt,' first published in 1975." Physically: "a deck of 7-by-9-centimetre (2.8 in × 3.5 in) printed cards in a black box." ([Wikipedia — Oblique Strategies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies))

> "Each card offers a challenging constraint intended to help artists (particularly musicians) break writer's block by encouraging lateral thinking." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies))

### Origin
- 1970: Peter Schmidt created "The Thoughts Behind the Thoughts", "a box containing 55 sentences letterpress printed onto disused prints that accumulated in his studio, which is still in Eno's possession." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies))
- Eno "had been pursuing a similar project himself, which he had handwritten onto a number of bamboo cards and given the name 'Oblique Strategies' in 1974." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies))
- "There was a significant overlap between the two projects, and so, in late 1974, Schmidt and Eno combined them into a single pack of cards and offered them for general sale." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies))

### How it's used — the actual instruction (this is the key mechanic)
From the introduction to the 2001 edition:
> "These cards evolved from separate observations of the principles underlying what we were doing. Sometimes they were recognised in retrospect (intellect catching up with intuition), sometimes they were identified as they were happening, sometimes they were formulated." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies))

> "They can be used as a pack, or by drawing a single card from the shuffled pack when a dilemma occurs in a working situation. In this case the card is trusted even if its appropriateness is quite unclear..." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies))

That final clause — **the card is trusted even if its appropriateness is quite unclear** — is the entire design philosophy in one sentence, and the single most quotable line for a prompt-deck feature.

Secondary commentary notes the deck's ambiguity about being an oracle at all: "It is not clear from any sources whether the cards were explicitly intended to be oracular at the outset - that is, whether or not Peter Schmidt and Eno necessarily saw them exclusively as a 'single instruction/single response' kind of 'game'." It also records a design detail worth copying: "the deck was not conceived of as a set of 'fixed' instructions, but rather a group of ideas to be added to or modified over time; each of the three decks included 4 or 5 blank cards, intended to be filled and used as needed." ([boot-boyz.biz — Oblique Strategies v2](https://boot-boyz.biz/products/oblique-strategies-v2)). The same page describes "a set of 115 white cards with simple black text in a deck subtitled Over One Hundred Worthwhile Dilemmas" and notes Schmidt "had just finished a set of 64 drawings based on the I Ching" ([boot-boyz.biz](https://boot-boyz.biz/products/oblique-strategies-v2)).

### What makes it gamified
Minimally, but deliberately:
- A **single random draw** with a **commitment rule** ("the card is trusted even if its appropriateness is quite unclear") — the only "mechanic" is agreeing in advance to obey.
- **Blank cards** in the physical deck: the user extends the system.
- **Deliberate ambiguity** — most cards are unresolvable without interpretation, which is the point.
- Cards divide into recognisable families: reversals ("Turn it upside down"), subtractions ("Simple subtraction", "Discard an axiom"), reframings ("Honour thy error as a hidden intention"), embodiment ("Ask your body", "Breathe more deeply"), and external perspective ("What would your closest friend do?", "How would you have done it?").

### Direct relevance to Lit Technica
The purest precedent for an *optional, attributed, non-mandatory* prompt: no scoring, no progression, one card at a time, invoked only when stuck. Two design lessons: (1) publish the **commitment framing** ("try it even if it seems irrelevant") because that's what makes an arbitrary prompt useful; (2) include **user-authored blank cards** so the deck grows with the writer. A "Revision lens" deck of manuscript-specific obliques (e.g. reversal, subtraction, amplify-the-embarrassing) is an obvious feature shape — the mechanics are public domain even though the card texts are not.

## 9. Blades in the Dark (John Harper) and Trophy (Jesse Ross)

### 9a. Blades in the Dark

**Source:** the Blades SRD, published by John Harper under Creative Commons; the copy fetched is a GitHub-hosted markdown mirror of the SRD text (**non-primary mirror of a CC-licensed SRD**). ([Blades in the Dark SRD (GitHub mirror)](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

**The Devil's Bargain** — voluntary cost for a bonus die:

> "PCs in *Blades* are reckless scoundrels addicted to destructive vices—they don't always act in their own best interests. To reflect this, the GM or any other player can offer you a bonus die if you accept a Devil's Bargain. Common Devil's Bargains include:
> - Collateral damage, unintended harm.
> - Sacrifice **coin** or an item.
> - Betray a friend or loved one.
> - Offend or anger a faction.
> - Start and/or tick a troublesome clock.
> - Add **heat** to the crew from evidence or witnesses.
> - Suffer harm." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

> "The Devil's Bargain occurs regardless of the outcome of the roll. You make the deal, pay the price, and get the bonus die." And: "The Devil's Bargain is always a free choice. If you don't like one, just reject it (or suggest how to alter it so you might consider taking it)." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

**Position and effect** — a two-axis framing device applied *before* the dice:

| | Controlled | Risky | Desperate |
|---|---|---|---|
| flavour | "*You act on your terms. You exploit a dominant advantage.*" | "*You go head to head. You act under fire. You take a chance.*" | "*You overreach your capabilities. You're in serious trouble.*" |
| 4/5 | "You hesitate… a **minor complication** occurs, you have **reduced effect**, you suffer **lesser harm**, you end up in a **risky** position." | "You do it, but there's a consequence: you suffer **harm**, a **complication** occurs, you have **reduced effect**, you end up in a **desperate** position." | "You do it, but there's a consequence: you suffer **severe harm**, a **serious complication** occurs, you have **reduced effect**." |
| 1-3 | "You falter. Press on by seizing a **risky** opportunity, or withdraw and try a different approach." | "Things go badly. You suffer **harm**, a **complication** occurs, you end up in a **desperate** position, you **lose this opportunity**." | "It's the worst outcome. You suffer **severe harm**, a **serious complication** occurs, you **lose this opportunity** for action." |

"**By default, an action roll is risky.** You wouldn't be rolling if there was no risk involved." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

Effect levels come with built-in reflective questions: "**GREAT** — *You achieve more than usual. How does the extra effort manifest? What additional benefit do you enjoy?* (3 ticks)"; "**STANDARD** — *You achieve what we'd expect as 'normal' with this action. Is that enough, or is there more left to do?* (2 ticks)"; "**LIMITED** — *You achieve a partial or weak effect. How is your impact diminished? What effort remains to achieve your goal?* (1 tick)" ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

**Progress clocks** — the mechanic most obviously portable to a writing app's plot tracker:

> "A **progress clock** is a circle divided into segments… Draw a progress clock when you need to track ongoing effort against an obstacle or the approach of impending trouble." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

> "A complex obstacle is a 4-segment clock. A more complicated obstacle is a 6-clock. A daunting obstacle is an 8-segment clock." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

> "When you create a clock, make it about the **obstacle,** not the method. The clocks for an infiltration should be 'Interior Patrols' and 'The Tower,' not 'Sneak Past the Guards' or 'Climb the Tower.'" ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

> "Remember that a clock tracks progress. It reflects the fictional situation, so the group can gauge how they're doing. A clock is like a speedometer in a car. It *shows* the speed of the vehicle—it doesn't determine the speed." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

Clock varieties, each a distinct plot-tension shape: **Danger clocks** ("the guards hunt down the intruders, activate an alarm, release the hounds"); **Racing clocks** ("The PCs might have a progress clock called 'Escape' while the constables have a clock called 'Cornered'"); **Linked clocks** ("she might have a clock for her 'Defense' and then a linked clock for 'Vulnerable'"); **Mission clocks** ("to represent the window of opportunity"); **Tug-of-war clocks** ("a 'Revolution!' clock that indicates when the refugees start to riot… Some events will tick the clock up and some will tick it down"); **Long-term projects** ("A basic long-term project… is eight segments"); and **Faction clocks** — "Each faction has a long-term goal. When the PCs have **downtime**, the GM ticks forward the faction clocks that they're interested in. In this way, the world around the PCs is dynamic and things happen that they're not directly connected to." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

**Flashbacks** — retroactive plotting with a price:
> "When an operation is underway, you can invoke a **flashback** to roll for an action in the past that impacts your current situation." Costs: "**0 Stress**: An ordinary action for which you had easy opportunity"; "**1 Stress**: A complex action or unlikely opportunity. *The Hound **Finessed** his pistols into a hiding spot near the card table so he could retrieve them after the pat-down at the front door.*"; "**2 (or more) Stress**: An elaborate action that involved special opportunities or contingencies." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

The limit is a good craft rule in itself: "A flashback isn't time travel. It can't 'undo' something that just occurred in the present moment." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

**Trauma conditions** — eight permanent character-change prompts triggered by exhaustion:
> "**Cold**: You're not moved by emotional appeals or social bonds. / **Haunted**: You're often lost in reverie, reliving past horrors, seeing things. / **Obsessed**: You're enthralled by one thing: an activity, a person, an ideology. / **Paranoid**: You imagine danger everywhere; you can't trust others. / **Reckless**: You have little regard for your own safety or best interests. / **Soft**: You lose your edge; you become sentimental, passive, gentle. / **Unstable**: Your emotional state is volatile. You can instantly rage, or fall into despair, act impulsively, or freeze up. / **Vicious**: You seek out opportunities to hurt people, even for no good reason." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

"**Trauma conditions are permanent**. Your character acquires the new personality quirk indicated by the condition, and can earn xp by using it to cause trouble." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

**Planning & engagement** — anti-planning-paralysis, and a direct answer to writers who over-outline:
> "But you, the players, don't have to do the nitty-gritty planning. The characters take care of that, off-screen. All you have to do is choose what **type of plan** the characters *have already made*." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

> "There are six different plans, each with a missing **detail** you need to provide:
> - Assault - Do violence to a target. *Detail: The point of attack.*
> - Deception - Lure, trick, or manipulate. *Detail: The method of deception.*
> - Stealth - Trespass unseen. *Detail: The point of infiltration.*
> - Occult - Engage a supernatural power. *Detail: The arcane method.*
> - Social - Negotiate, bargain, or persuade. *Detail: The social connection.*
> - Transport - Carry cargo or people through danger. *Detail: The route and means.*" ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

The **engagement roll** then jumps straight to the first obstacle: "starting with **1d for sheer luck**", modified by questions like "Is this operation particularly bold or daring? Take **+1d**. Is this operation overly complex or contingent on many factors? Take **-1d**." Results: Critical "Exceptional result…", 6 "You're in a controlled position when the action starts.", 4/5 "You're in a risky position when the action starts.", 1-3 "You're in a desperate position when the action starts." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

And a clean scene-transition rule: "Don't make the engagement roll and then describe the PCs *approaching* the target. It's the approach that the engagement roll resolves. Cut to the action that results *because of* that initial approach—to the first serious obstacle in their path." ([Blades SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))

### 9b. Trophy (Jesse Ross) — Trophy Dark / Trophy Gold

**Source:** the official Trophy SRD on the publisher's own site — **primary**. ([Trophy RPG — System / SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

**Light and dark dice:**
> "Games Rooted in Trophy tend to use two different colors of six-sided dice: light dice and dark dice. Light dice are typically less risky, whereas dark dice are typically more risky." ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

**The Risk Roll** — note the opening ritual, which is a prompt in itself:
> "When you attempt a risky task, say what you hope will happen and ask the GM and the other players what could possibly go wrong. Then gather 6-sided dice." ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

Dice gathering, verbatim: "Take one *light* die if the task is something you would be able to do because of one of your Skills or piece of equipment… Take another *light* die for accepting a Devil's Bargain from another player or the GM… Add a *dark* die if you are risking your mind or body to attempt the task." ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

| Highest die | Result |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | "You fail, and things get worse. The GM describes how, which may or may not be connected to one of the ideas offered when you asked what could go wrong." |
| 4–5 | "You succeed, but there's some kind of complication. The GM describes the complication, then you describe how you succeed, or vice versa." |
| 6 | "You succeed. Describe how, or ask the GM to describe it." |

> "If your **highest** die is a dark die, and the dark die is **higher** than your current Ruin, mark 1 Ruin." And the push-your-luck clause: "If you are unhappy with your roll and your highest die is a light die, you may add a *dark* die to your dice pool and re-roll them all. You may keep adding a dark die and re-rolling until you're satisfied with your result or until your highest die is a dark die." Plus: "When dark and light dice are tied, the dark die is considered the highest." ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

**Trophy's Devil's Bargains** (a shorter, more setting-neutral list than Blades'): "Causing collateral damage or unintended harm / Getting lost or separated from your companions / Sacrificing an item or piece of treasure / Betraying another character / Attracting unwanted attention" ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

**Ruin — the escalation counter:**
> "Your Ruin shows how much physical and mental harm your character has suffered. It starts at 1." / "Ruin is a number between 1 and 6 which tracks a character's descent. Nearly every roll that a player makes has a chance to affect their character's Ruin. At 6 Ruin, a character is removed from play." ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

The betrayal valve: "When you mark 5 Ruin, you may now start to clear Ruin by betraying or sabotaging the other characters. You should do these acts in a way that does not draw attention to yourself. The more it looks like an accident or simple bad luck, the better." ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

The end: "When you mark your last Ruin—meaning all 6 Ruin boxes are marked—you lose yourself to the wilds that have been growing inside you. You choose whether you become a monster in service to the forces of nature (and the whims of the GM), or whether you simply die." ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

**Conditions** — transformation prompts attached to each Ruin increase. Examples given verbatim: "Sprained ankle / Slowly becoming petrified / Thinks companions have been body-swapped / Black-and-white vision / Possessed by a ghost" ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

**Weak Point** (combat): "First say how you expose yourself to injury or attack, then roll a single *light* die. The number on the die is called your Weak Point, and represents the risks you're taking to face the monster." Monsters have an Endurance "between 2 and 12"; "If the total of the **two highest** dark dice is **equal to or higher** than the Endurance, the monster is defeated in the manner you and your fellow players describe." ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

**Trophy Dark's scenario architecture** (from a detailed published review — **secondary source**, used because the official Trophy Dark pages returned HTTP errors during this session):
> "The 22 incursions (or scenarios) included with the text are broken into 5 stages called rings–likely inspired by tree rings. They feature different terrors and temptations you can throw at your players. After your players have encountered a temptation and a terror, they move deeper into the rings–even if there are remaining encounters." ([Cassi Mothwin — "The Forest Hates You: A Review of Trophy Dark"](https://cassimothwin.com/2023/11/29/the-forest-hates-you-a-review-of-trophy-dark/))

> "Each incursion has a one-word theme. 'The Gift of the Sea' by Gabriel Robinson uses the theme of TIDE and calls upon that theme throughout. Conditions that line the bottom of the scenario might make a player 'feel anxious at the thought of being out of the water' or all sounds will become mute, 'as if you're underwater.' Each ring also focuses on this theme… If GMs aren't sure how to move a session further, the text suggests falling back on the theme word and using it to guide the players forward." ([Cassi Mothwin review](https://cassimothwin.com/2023/11/29/the-forest-hates-you-a-review-of-trophy-dark/))

Character creation: "With a few simple rolls on the evocative character generation tables (PDF warning), it's easy to dive in as a player. ### 9c. Correction — the escalation die is 13th Age, not Blades

The brief pairs "escalation die" with Blades in the Dark. That attribution is incorrect: the escalation die is a **13th Age** mechanic. Verbatim:

> "The escalation die represents a bonus to attacks as the fight goes on. At the start of the second round, the GM sets the escalation die at 1. Each PC gains a bonus to attack rolls equal to the current value on the escalation die. Each round, the escalation die advances by +1, to a maximum of +6. Monsters and NPCs do not add the escalation die bonus to their attacks." ([13th Age SRD — Combat Rules](https://www.13thagesrd.com/combat-rules/))

And the anti-stalling clause, which is the interesting part for pacing design: "If the GM judges that the characters are avoiding conflict rather than bringing the fight to the bad guys, the escalation die doesn't advance. If combat virtually ceases, the escalation die resets to 0." ([13th Age SRD](https://www.13thagesrd.com/combat-rules/)) Also: "Fear dazes you and prevents you from using the escalation die." ([13th Age SRD](https://www.13thagesrd.com/combat-rules/))

Blades' equivalents to "escalation" are the **progress clock** (fills toward a fixed event) and **stress → trauma**; Trophy's is **Ruin** (1→6, terminal).

### What makes these gamified
- **Voluntary cost-for-advantage** (Devil's Bargain) — the bargain resolves regardless of the roll, so it is a genuine trade, and it is always refusable.
- **Position/effect framing before resolution** — the outcome space is negotiated in fiction before dice are touched.
- **Filling clocks** as a visible, non-binary progress bar for both PC goals and offscreen faction goals.
- **Escalation counters** that only move one way: Trophy's Ruin, Blades' trauma track, 13th Age's escalation die.
- **Push-your-luck re-rolls** (Trophy: add a dark die and re-roll, increasing both success odds and Ruin risk).
- **Retroactive authoring priced in a resource** (flashbacks cost stress).
- **Permanent character-change tables** (Trauma conditions; Trophy Conditions) triggered by mechanical exhaustion rather than player choice.

### Direct relevance to Lit Technica
Three transferable devices. (1) **Progress clocks** as a Plot/Timeline widget — a named 4/6/8-segment obstacle that the writer ticks as scenes land, including "faction clocks" for offscreen forces that advance while the protagonist is elsewhere; the "make it about the obstacle, not the method" rule is a real craft insight. (2) **Devil's Bargain** as a scene-level prompt: "take this complication and it happens no matter how the scene resolves" — an optional way to add cost to an easy beat. (3) **Position/effect** as a pre-scene lens: is this character acting from a controlled, risky, or desperate position, and is the achievable effect great, standard, or limited? Those two questions alone will restructure a flat scene.

## Cross-cutting patterns (synthesis for the Lit Technica prompt-deck feature)

**Six recurring mechanic families**, each independently attested above:

1. **Binary oracle with declared odds** — Ironsworn's Ask the Oracle. The user states expected likelihood; the system answers Yes/No with an occasional twist. Cheap to build, highly reusable across all four workspaces.
2. **Word-pair ambiguity generators** — Ironsworn/Starforged Action+Theme and Descriptor+Focus; Story Cubes' nine icons. Output is deliberately under-determined and requires interpretation.
3. **Two-option branching prompts** — The Quiet Year's card structure ("or…"). Randomness delivers a dilemma, not an answer; the writer still chooses.
4. **Fill-in-the-blank relational templates** — Dungeon World Bonds ("[name] knows incriminating details about me."). Generates inter-character tension in one line.
5. **Escalating countdowns** — Blades' progress clocks, DW's grim portents, Trophy's Ruin, The Quiet Year's project dice, 13th Age's escalation die. All make offscreen pressure visible and monotonic.
6. **Constrained output formats** — TYOV's "[event]; [feeling/action]" single sentence; The Quiet Year's 30-second drawings and 1–2 sentence arguments; Story Engine's 5-minute writing sprint. Anti-perfectionism devices as much as prompts.

**Three framings that make optional prompts actually work**, all quotable with attribution:
- Eno/Schmidt: "the card is trusted even if its appropriateness is quite unclear" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies)).
- Story Engine: "Ignore any card, rule, or guideline that doesn't help you create." ([Story Engine Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf)).
- Story Cubes: "There is one rule: there are no wrong answers." ([Zygomatic rules](https://www.zygomatic-games.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/storycubesclassic_en_rules.pdf)).

**Two structural ideas worth prototyping first:**
- **Locking** (Story Engine) — bind a generated prompt to an entity the writer already has in their manuscript. This is what separates an in-app prompt deck from a generic prompt website.
- **Stakes questions** (Dungeon World) — "A good stakes question is one that, when it's resolved, means that things will never be the same again" plus the commitment rule "once you've written it as a stake, it's out of your hands" ([DW SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/)). A Plot workspace could hold 1–3 open stakes questions per act.

**Licensing note for product work:** the Dungeon World SRD, Ironsworn SRD, Blades in the Dark SRD, Trophy SRD and Traveller SRD are all published under open/CC or open-game licences and their *mechanics* are broadly reusable with attribution; Microscope, The Quiet Year, Thousand Year Old Vampire, The Story Engine Deck, Deck of Worlds, Rory's Story Cubes and Oblique Strategies are conventionally copyrighted commercial products whose prompt *text* must not be reproduced. Verify each licence directly before shipping anything derived from it — that verification was not part of this research.
