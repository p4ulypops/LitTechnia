# Worldbuilding, Character & Escalation Prompts — Craft Research

**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Feeds:** Lit Technica's Craft Guidance library (P1 feature), specifically the Worldbuilding, Character, Plot and Timeline workspaces. Companion to [`lit-technica-craft-research-2026-08-04.md`](./lit-technica-craft-research-2026-08-04.md) (author-interview craft research) and the [PRD](./lit-technica-prd-v0.1.md).

**Brief:** research prompts and gamification techniques that help writers build worlds and characters, anchored on two example prompt patterns supplied by the requester:

1. A **tech-level / "most advanced option" checklist**, e.g. *"When [travelling by transport | gaining healthcare | bored], what is the most advanced form of technology?"*
2. An **escalation / stakes test**, e.g. *"If this scene carries on for another few pages, would this change the situation?"*

Both patterns turn out to have direct published precedents (sections 1 and 3 below), and both sit inside a much larger family of question banks, TTRPG-derived random-prompt mechanics, and app-gamification design principles, covered in full below.

---

## 1. Worldbuilding question banks

### 1a. The tech-level / "most advanced option" pattern — direct precedent

The **Weltenbau Wissen List of Worldbuilding Questions** asks almost exactly this, twice, in its Society/Infrastructure section:

- Q72 (transport): *"What means of transport exist, which are particularly progressive, which ones are commonplace and widely available?"*
- Q73 (healthcare): *"How far has medicine progressed, which diseases and injuries can be treated successfully?"*

([The Weltenbau Wissen List of Worldbuilding Questions, PDF](http://www.weltenbau-wissen.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/The_Weltenbau_Wissen_List_of_Worldbuilding_Questions.pdf) — licensed CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, see licensing note below)

**Write On Sisters'** worldbuilding checklist reframes the same idea as a single generative question applied across every life domain: *"WHY are things like this?"* — with a worked example that lands on the same healthcare axis: *"Is there health care? What happens if someone gets sick or injured?"* ([Write On Sisters — 1 Key Question for Worldbuilding](http://writeonsisters.com/writing-craft/1-key-question-for-worldbuilding-a-handy-checklist/))

This confirms the requester's example is not a one-off invention but a recognized worldbuilding move: **take any life domain (transport, healthcare, boredom/leisure, communication, warfare, death) and ask what the most advanced available option is, then ask who can't access it and why.** That second half — access, not just existence — is the detail both sources add that's easy to miss on a first pass, and it is the more useful prompt for characterization (a world's *most advanced* medicine is far less story-relevant than who is priced or politically excluded from it).

### 1b. Patricia C. Wrede's "Fantasy Worldbuilding Questions"

The most widely cited worldbuilding question bank in the genre: roughly 60 questions grouped as Geography, History, Magic, Government/Law, Economy, **Technology**, **Transport**, **Medicine**, Arts, Customs, Language, and Daily Life. Wrede's own framing for how to use it: *"If it's useful, use it. If not, don't."* ([Wrede — Fantasy Worldbuilding Questions: Daily Life](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-daily-life/); SFWA reprints organized by topic, e.g. [The World](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-the-world/), [Commerce, Trade and Public Life](https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-commerce-trade-and-public-life/))

**Licensing flag:** Wrede's list is conventionally copyrighted; the SFWA reprint is explicitly marked "reprinted with author's permission." **Reproduction and distribution is specifically prohibited** without her permission — do not surface her question text verbatim in-app; paraphrase the topic categories only, with attribution.

### 1c. Chaotic Shiny — randomizer as taxonomy

Not a question bank but a set of field-based randomizer generators (Civilization, Law, Culture, etc.) — useful less as prompt text and more as a **ready-made field taxonomy** for a Worldbuilding data model, e.g. Culture fields "Highly Values" / "Major Taboo" / "Major Social Ill." ([Chaotic Shiny — Civilization Generator](http://chaoticshiny.com/civgen.php), [Law Generator](http://chaoticshiny.com/lawgen.php))

### 1d. SFWA craft articles

- "Crafting Diverse Cultures" — applies Trompenaars' cross-cultural dimensions (universalism/particularism, individualism/communitarianism, etc.) as worldbuilding interrogation questions ([SFWA — A Worldbuilding Guide to Crafting Diverse Cultures](https://sfwa.org/2020/01/31/a-worldbuilding-guide-to-crafting-diverse-cultures/))
- "Ecology of Worldbuilding" — food-web and resource-dependency prompts ([SFWA — Ecology of Worldbuilding](https://sfwa.org/2022/10/25/ecology-worldbuilding/))
- "The Art of Story as Worldbuilding" — a restraint-oriented counter-principle: *"Don't explain it unless you must."* ([SFWA — The Art of Story as Worldbuilding](https://sfwa.org/2019/10/16/the-art-of-story-as-worldbuilding/))

### 1e. MICE quotient (Orson Scott Card)

Milieu / Idea / Character / Event — a diagnostic rather than a prompt bank: identify which of the four is your story's primary thread, then check that its opening and closing match (a Milieu story ends when the character leaves the place; an Idea story ends when the question is answered; etc.). Useful as a structural sanity check layered on top of worldbuilding output. ([Writing Excuses 6.10 — Scott Card's M.I.C.E. Quotient](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-10-scott-cards-m-i-c-e-quotient/); [Nate Listrom — MICE Internal/External](https://www.natelistrom.com/2022/11/13/mice-internal-external.html))

### 1f. Pitfall checks (not generative, but worth surfacing as warnings)

TV Tropes' [World Building](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WorldBuilding), [Info Dump](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/InfoDump) and [As You Know](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AsYouKnow) pages catalogue common worldbuilding-exposition failure modes. These are better framed as an optional "check your draft" lens than a prompt deck.

## 2. Character prompts

- **Gotham Writers Character Questionnaire** — basics plus an "unconventional" physicality tier, e.g. *"What is in your character's refrigerator right now?"* ([Gotham Writers — Character Questionnaire](https://www.writingclasses.com/toolbox/character-questionnaire/gotham))
- **Proust Questionnaire (character-adapted)** — second-person interiority prompts, e.g. *"What is your greatest fear?"* ([Gotham Writers — Proust Questionnaire](https://www.writingclasses.com/toolbox/character-questionnaire/proust))
- **Dungeon World Bonds** — fill-in-the-blank relational templates that generate inter-character tension in one line, e.g. *"[name] knows incriminating details about me."* / *"I have conned [name]."* ([Dungeon World SRD — Thief](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/thief/))
- **Dungeon World Look** — pick-one-from-each visual menus, e.g. *"Hooded, scarred, or disheveled face / Sharp eyes, dead eyes, or calculating eyes"* ([Dungeon World SRD — Thief](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/thief/))
- **Thousand Year Old Vampire's Experience template** — a compact one-sentence format for a backstory beat: *"[description of the event]; [how I feel or what I did about it]"* ([Thousand Year Old Vampire v1.04](https://pdfcoffee.com/tyovver104pdf-pdf-free.html) — non-primary mirror)
- **Traveller Lifepath** — character generation as a push-your-luck mini-game across life "terms," where failure (a mishap) is the generative output, not a penalty: a failed survival roll yields *"1d6 Contacts and 1d3 Enemies"* or an unexplained gap in memory rather than ending the character. ([Traveller SRD — Careers](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/careers/))

## 3. Plot / scene escalation prompts

### 3a. The "if this scene continued" pattern — direct precedents

The requester's second example — *"If this scene carries on for another few pages, would this change the situation?"* — is a close match to two published craft tools:

- **Robert McKee's stakes test:** *"What is the risk? … what's the worst thing that will happen to the protagonist if he does not achieve his desire?"* ([McKee, Story — Style, Structure, Substance, and the Principles of Screenwriting, PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5af1145d697a987d247c2773/t/5f134b8555e4ba6ef215f38a/1595100062763/Story-+Style,+Structure,+Substance,+and+the+Principles+of+Screenwriting+by+Robert+McKee.pdf))
- **Savannah Gilbo's "Progressive Complications"** — a 5-step scene diagnostic: rank each complication in the scene 1–10 for severity, verify each new complication scores higher than the last (true escalation, not repetition), and identify the scene's turning point. This is the closest published match to the requester's exact framing — it literally asks whether the scene's pressure is still rising as it continues, or has gone flat. ([Savannah Gilbo — Progressive Complications](https://www.savannahgilbo.com/blog/progressive-complications))
- **Jami Gold on raising stakes** — practical technique notes on the same problem ([Writers Helping Writers — What Does It Mean to Raise the Stakes?](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2017/03/what-does-it-mean-to-raise-the-stakes/))
- **K.M. Weiland's "ten worst things" exercise** — brainstorm the ten worst things that could happen to a character next, then use the list to escalate (via secondary source: [One Wild Word — Frustrate Your Characters to Keep Readers Turning Pages](https://onewildword.com/2013/09/30/frustrate-your-characters-to-keep-readers-turning-pages/))

### 3b. TTRPG mechanics that operationalize the same test

Several tabletop game systems turn "is this scene/threat still escalating?" into a literal mechanic rather than a question the writer has to self-assess — these are strong candidates for a lightweight visual widget rather than just prose guidance:

- **Dungeon World Fronts / stakes questions** — *"A good stakes question is one that, when it's resolved, means that things will never be the same again"* and the commitment rule *"once you've written it as a stake, it's out of your hands, you don't get to just make it up anymore"* ([Dungeon World SRD — Fronts](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/))
- **Blades in the Dark progress clocks** — a segmented dial (4/6/8-segment) that visibly fills as pressure builds; the design rule *"make it about the obstacle, not the method"* is a genuine craft insight for scene tension ([Blades in the Dark SRD](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md))
- **13th Age's escalation die** — a bonus that climbs +1 each round the conflict continues, explicitly reset if the characters stall: *"If combat virtually ceases, the escalation die resets to 0"* ([13th Age SRD — Combat Rules](https://www.13thagesrd.com/combat-rules/))
- **Trophy's Ruin** — a one-way 1→6 counter tracking cumulative cost; hitting 6 ends the character ([Trophy SRD](https://trophyrpg.com/system/))

All four give the same underlying answer to "would continuing this scene change the situation?": if you can't point to a clock ticking, a die climbing, or a stakes question edging toward resolution, the scene is flat and needs either a real complication or a cut.

## 4. Gamified TTRPG mechanics mapped to Lit Technica workspaces

Full sourced detail on all nine systems researched (Microscope, The Quiet Year, Thousand Year Old Vampire, Ironsworn/Starforged, Dungeon World, Traveller, Story Engine Deck/Deck of Worlds/Story Cubes, Oblique Strategies, Blades in the Dark/Trophy/13th Age) lives in the underlying research file; the six recurring mechanic families and their most direct in-app translation are:

| Mechanic family | Best exemplar | Lit Technica translation |
|---|---|---|
| Binary oracle with declared odds | Ironsworn's Ask the Oracle: pick Almost Certain / Likely / 50-50 / Unlikely / Small Chance, then roll ([Ironsworn SRD](https://tedtschopp.github.io/Ironsworn-SRD/Ironsworn%20SRD.html)) | A "resolve a plot question" widget usable in any workspace when the writer is stuck on a yes/no |
| Word-pair ambiguity generators | Starforged's Action+Theme / Descriptor+Focus paired oracles ([SwornForged SRD](https://swornforged.com/srd/oracles)) | A low-friction "Spark an idea" button for Worldbuilding, returning a deliberately under-determined verb+noun pair to interpret |
| Two-option branching prompts | The Quiet Year's card structure, each card offering two alternative prompts | A prompt deck where randomness delivers a dilemma, not an answer — the writer still chooses |
| Fill-in-the-blank relational templates | Dungeon World Bonds ([DW SRD — Thief](https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/thief/)) | A Character-workspace relationship-prompt deck |
| Escalating countdowns | Blades' progress clocks, 13th Age's escalation die, Trophy's Ruin, DW's grim portents | A Plot/Timeline "tension clock" widget — directly answers the requester's escalation-prompt pattern with a visible dial instead of a self-check question |
| Constrained output formats | TYOV's one-sentence Experience template; Quiet Year's 30-second drawings; Story Engine's 5-minute writing sprint | Anti-perfectionism output caps paired with any prompt, to keep prompts fast rather than another blank-page problem |

**Two structural ideas worth prototyping first**, both from Peter Chiykowski's Story Engine Deck / Deck of Worlds:

- **Locking** — binding a generated prompt to an entity the writer already has in their manuscript, e.g. tucking a card facedown to represent an existing character or place rather than generating a new one from scratch. This is what would separate an in-app prompt deck from a generic prompt-of-the-day website. ([Story Engine Deck Guidebook, PDF](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))
- **Deck of Worlds' Origin / Attribute / Advent triad** — a built-in past/present/future schema for any setting element (a region, a landmark, a faction), ready-made for Worldbuilding entries that want a timeline dimension without inventing one from scratch ([Deck of Worlds Guidebook, PDF](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/Deck-of-Worlds-Guidebook.pdf))

**Three quotable framings that explain why optional, non-mandatory prompts work at all** — useful for in-app copy near any prompt-deck feature:

- Eno/Schmidt (Oblique Strategies): *"the card is trusted even if its appropriateness is quite unclear"* ([Wikipedia — Oblique Strategies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies))
- Story Engine Deck: *"Ignore any card, rule, or guideline that doesn't help you create."* ([Story Engine Deck Guidebook](https://files.storyenginedeck.com/The-Story-Engine-Deck-Guidebook.pdf))
- Rory's Story Cubes: *"There is one rule: there are no wrong answers."* ([Zygomatic — Story Cubes Classic rules, PDF](https://www.zygomatic-games.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/storycubesclassic_en_rules.pdf))

## 5. Gamification design principles ("progress without shame")

Eight principles synthesized from eight comparator apps/frameworks, directly relevant if any gamified layer is added around the prompt library — consistent with the PRD's existing "progress without shame" product principle (no punitive streaks/quotas/completion percentages):

1. **Make the game layer optional and switchable.** Habitica's HP/Gold/party-damage system is the clearest anti-pattern: missing a task can cost a level, all gold, and damage every party member, and reviewers report players "genuinely" find it "punishing" enough that "players admit they 'cheat'" ([HabitSlayer — Habitica review](https://habitslayer.com/guides/habitica-review); [Habitica Party wiki](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Party)).
2. **Pair a never-resetting cumulative counter with any resettable streak.** 4 The Words tracks three separate counters — Current Streak, Longest Streak, and **Total Streak Days, which never resets** — so a broken streak doesn't erase all prior effort ([4 The Words — Streaks overview](https://help.4thewords.com/writing/writing-streaks/overview)).
3. **Build in free forgiveness.** 4 The Words offers a free same-day streak repair, explicitly designed to "value 'showing up' for writing, even if that means not writing" that day ([4 The Words — Streak Repairs](https://4thewords.tawk.help/article/streak-repairs-reserve)).
4. **Let the user set their own target rather than imposing a universal one.** Written? Kitten! lets the user pick their own reward threshold (100/200/500/1000 words) with pure reward and no penalty ([Fiction Writers Review — Written? Kitten!](https://fictionwritersreview.com/shoptalk/written-kitten/)); Scrivener auto-calculates targets from the user's own deadline and writing days rather than a fixed quota ([Literature & Latte — Track Statistics and Targets](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/track-statistics-and-targets-in-your-scrivener-projects)).
5. **Reward showing up, not just completion.** NaNoWriMo's fixed 1,667-words/day quota has been criticized as an arbitrary, unsustainable vanity metric, with commentators noting the org's real strength was community rather than the quota mechanic itself ([critical review — Beyond 50,000 Words](https://www.howdycuriosity.com/blogs/curated-corner/beyond-50-000-words-a-critical-look-at-nanowrimos-impact-on-writers-and-the-industry); the organization itself dissolved 31 March 2025, per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Novel_Writing_Month)).
6. **Frame progress as informational, not controlling — no failure-triggered nagging.** Duolingo's guilt-toned streak-loss notifications have been documented as "emotional blackmail" by users ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/duolingo-meanest-app-nagging-notifications-melting-icon-gen-z-marketing-2024-7)), even though a large randomized study found streaks raised achievement 0.13–0.17 standard deviations with no measurable discouragement after a break ([NBER Working Paper 34173, PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34173/w34173.pdf)) — the same paper notes Khan Academy retired streaks as "demotivating," so the net effect is genuinely mixed and framing matters more than the mechanic itself.
7. **Prefer collaboration over competition; avoid public shortfall displays.** Academic research on badges/leaderboards is mixed: Hanus & Fox (2015) found they decreased motivation, satisfaction, and exam scores in a classroom setting ([Arizona SDT review, PDF](https://journals.librarypublishing.arizona.edu/itlt/article/id/4872/download/pdf/)); Mekler et al. (2017) found no measurable harm and increased task volume. Self-determination theory predicts rewards backfire most for already-intrinsically-motivated users, which describes most first-time novelists.
8. **Treat lapses as a record, not a verdict.** Forest keeps dead trees (missed focus sessions) visible as "an honest record of the journey… not a verdict or a source of shame" rather than deleting them ([forestapp.cc](https://www.forestapp.cc/)) — the best model found for how a Lit Technica progress view should display a skipped day or an abandoned prompt.
